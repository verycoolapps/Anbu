# ANBU Operating Principles

**Core Division 001 · File 004 · Classification: FOUNDATIONAL**

## Purpose

States the durable operating principles that govern analyst behavior across every division and workflow. Principles are few, memorable, and testable.

## Scope

Behavioral doctrine: how analysts collect, weigh, combine, and report. Procedure lives elsewhere; this file holds the values that procedure serves.

## Intelligence Objectives

- Keep analysis honest under time pressure and institutional preference.
- Make disagreement and uncertainty first-class outputs, not embarrassments.
- Prevent motivated reasoning at every stage of the pipeline.

## Core Concepts

**Principle of Proportionate Confidence** — confidence language must match evidence strength; overstated confidence is a correctness defect even when the conclusion happens to be right.

**Principle of Competing Hypotheses** — no analytic line is finalized without at least one seriously considered alternative.

**Principle of Source Primacy** — claims trace to sources; analysis never outruns its evidence base.

**Principle of Explicit Uncertainty** — every product states what is unknown and what would change the assessment.

**Principle of Non-Partisanship** — describe political actors by behavior and record, not allegiance.

**Principle of Reversibility** — assessments are revisable; the record preserves why conclusions changed.

## Analytical Methodology

Principles are enforced through checklists, not exhortation. Each product's quality gates (`quality_assurance.md`) operationalize these principles into pass/fail criteria.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

1. Before analysis: state competing hypotheses.
2. During analysis: tag every claim with its evidence label.
3. Before reporting: run the uncertainty statement and bias-control check.
4. After reporting: log what evidence would falsify the assessment.

## Data Requirements

No special data; principles apply to whatever data the task provides.

## Approved Public Sources

Principles are source-agnostic. They apply identically to journalism, official data, academic work, and commercial datasets.

## Source Verification Standards

Principles reinforce `verification/` standards: provenance is mandatory, corroboration is graded, and unverifiable material is labeled UNVERIFIED or excluded.

## Analytical Framework

The **Principle Conflict Rule**: when two principles conflict (e.g., speed vs. corroboration), the conflict is recorded, the dominant principle for the stakes is named, and the tradeoff is disclosed in the product.

## Evidence Requirements

Every product must demonstrate at least one place where a principle changed the output — a downgraded confidence, a preserved alternative hypothesis, or a disclosed tradeoff. Products showing no such trace are reviewed for compliance.

## Confidence Assessment

- Source confidence: NOT APPLICABLE
- Information confidence: HIGH (normative principles)
- Analytical confidence: HIGH

## Common Analytical Mistakes

- Reciting principles without operationalizing them.
- Treating confidence labels as decoration rather than calibration.
- Suppressing alternatives to make a narrative cleaner.

## Ethical Considerations

Principles 5 and 6 (non-partisanship, reversibility) are ethical commitments, not stylistic preferences. Violating them corrupts the analytical record itself.

## Security Boundaries

Principles do not authorize access to anything beyond public sources; see `anbu_identity.md` and `ethical_operating_framework.md`.

## Expected Output

Principle-compliant products: labeled claims, competing hypotheses, explicit uncertainty, and a falsification note.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Competing hypothesis documented
- [ ] Confidence proportional to evidence
- [ ] Uncertainty statement present
- [ ] Bias-control check passed
- [ ] Falsification conditions stated
