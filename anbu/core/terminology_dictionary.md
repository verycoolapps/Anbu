# ANBU Terminology Dictionary

**Core Division 001 · File 014 · Classification: REFERENCE**

## Purpose

General analytical and intelligence vocabulary used across ANBU products — broader than the ANBU-specific glossary. Covers tradecraft terms, source types, and analytic concepts drawn from public professional usage.

## Scope

Working vocabulary for analysts. Terms are defined for consistent use in ANBU products; they do not assert doctrinal authority beyond ANBU.

## Intelligence Objectives

- Give analysts and consumers a shared professional vocabulary.
- Distinguish common usage from ANBU-specific usage where they differ.
- Support precise, non-sloppy analytical writing.

## Core Concepts

### Collection & Sources

- **OSINT** — Open Source Intelligence: intelligence produced from publicly available information.
- **Primary source** — first-hand record: official document, dataset, direct observation, original journalism.
- **Secondary source** — analysis or reporting built on primary sources.
- **Tertiary source** — reference works, encyclopedias, aggregated summaries.
- **Human source (HUMINT)** — information from people; in ANBU limited to what people publish publicly.
- **SIGINT/IMINT/OSINT boundary** — ANBU handles OSINT and public IMINT only; it does not collect signals or imagery beyond public release.

### Analysis

- **Indicators** — observable signs that something is happening or likely to happen.
- **Warning indicators** — specific, observable conditions that precede an event.
- **Base rate** — how often something happens generally; needed to judge whether a specific case is unusual.
- **Mirror imaging** — assuming an actor thinks like you; a named bias ANBU corrects for.
- **Deception** — deliberate misleading; assessed via the `verification/` division.
- **Estimates vs. facts** — estimates are graded judgments; facts are labeled observations.

### Organizations & Systems

- **Bureaucracy** — the rule-bound machinery implementing policy; ANBU's second object of study.
- **Principal–agent problem** — divergence between what principals order and what agents do.
- **Institutional capacity** — the demonstrated ability of an institution to execute its mandate.
- **Veto player** — an actor whose agreement is necessary for a decision.
- **Bureaucratic politics** — competition within institutions that shapes outcomes independent of formal policy.

### Geopolitics & Security

- **Deterrence** — preventing action by threatening costs.
- **Compellence** — forcing action by threatening costs.
- **Gray-zone activity** — actions below the threshold of armed conflict.
- **Supply chain exposure** — dependence on foreign inputs, a standard analytic lens.
- **Threat actor** — an entity conducting hostile activity; described by capability and intent, never targeted.

## Analytical Methodology

Terms are used precisely: if a product says "verified," it means the ANBU label VERIFIED, not colloquial certainty. Ambiguous terms are replaced with glossary entries.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

1. Draft product using precise vocabulary.
2. Check terms against this dictionary and `intelligence_glossary.md`.
3. Replace colloquialisms and ambiguous words.
4. Ensure every specialized term is used consistently.

## Common Analytical Mistakes

- Using "intelligence" to mean both the product and the discipline.
- Using "terrorist" as an analytical label rather than a legal/designation term.
- Confusing "capability" (what an actor can do) with "intent" (what it wants to do) — always separate.

## Ethical Considerations

Vocabulary choices carry weight: labeling groups, regimes, or individuals requires the same evidence discipline as factual claims.

## Security Boundaries
