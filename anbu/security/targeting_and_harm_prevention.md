# Targeting and Harm Prevention

**Module:** ANBU OSINT — Security & Operational Protection
**Classification handling:** Apply research data classification rules to all outputs produced under this procedure. All stored artifacts must carry an analyst-assigned classification label.

## 1. Purpose
Prevent research outputs and processes from enabling harm to individuals or groups — ensuring that intelligence findings are not used for targeting, harassment, physical harm, or persecution, and that the research itself does not create targeting risk through data exposure or misuse.

## 2. Scope
All research subjects, data subjects, and downstream consumers of ANBU outputs — covering both deliberate misuse prevention and inadvertent harm creation.

## 3. Intelligence objectives
- Prevent ANBU products from being used as targeting tools.
- Prevent research data from enabling harm if compromised or misused.
- Ensure findings about vulnerable individuals or groups are handled with heightened care.
- Maintain clear refusal criteria for harmful requests.

## 4. Core concepts
- Harm taxonomy: physical harm, harassment, persecution, discrimination, reputational destruction, and psychological harm.
- Vulnerable-subject flagging: children, victims of crimes, whistleblowers, and at-risk populations receive maximum protection.
- Output screening: findings are reviewed for targeting potential before release.
- Refusal authority: analysts may refuse tasks whose primary purpose is enabling harm, with refusal documented.

## 5. Analytical methodology
1. At tasking, assess harm potential: could this research enable targeting or harm?
2. Flag vulnerable subjects for elevated handling.
3. During analysis, avoid generating targeting-enabling products (e.g., 'how to find' lists, vulnerability assessments of individuals).
4. Screen outputs for harm potential before release.
5. Document refusals and escalations.
6. On discovery of downstream misuse, escalate per safety escalation protocol.

## 6. Step-by-step workflow
1. Tasking harm assessment → 2. Vulnerable-subject flagging → 3. Analysis with harm-awareness → 4. Output harm screening → 5. Release decision → 6. Misuse monitoring → 7. Escalation on discovery.

## 7. Data requirements
Harm-potential assessment per task: who could be harmed, how, and with what data. Output screening records: reviewer assessment of targeting potential. Refusal log: task refused, reason, authority.

## 8. Approved public sources
Research on violent extremist organizations, threat actors, and criminal networks is legitimate when it serves prevention, documentation, or accountability — but outputs must serve those purposes, not enable replication or targeting of victims.

## 9. Source verification standards
Harm screening asks: does this output primarily inform defense/accountability, or does it primarily enable harm? Outputs serving both require careful scoping to the defensive purpose.

## 10. Analytical framework
Refusal criteria: requests whose primary purpose is harming a specific person or group, facilitating persecution, enabling doxxing, or providing targeting intelligence for violence. Borderline cases escalate to senior review, not silent compliance.

## 11. Evidence requirements
Harm-prevention register: tasks assessed, vulnerable flags, output screenings, refusals, and escalations — auditable.

## 12. Confidence assessment
HIGH: harm assessment at tasking, output screening current, zero harmful outputs released. MODERATE: assessment present, screening informal. LOW: harm potential unassessed. INSUFFICIENT: no harm prevention.

## 13. Common analytical mistakes
Treating 'interesting' as sufficient justification for harmful outputs; ignoring vulnerability of subjects; assuming downstream users will use findings responsibly; failing to refuse harmful tasks; releasing data that enables persecution of a group.

## 14. Ethical considerations
Intelligence exists to prevent harm and support accountability — not to create targeting lists. The moment research output's primary utility is enabling harm to a person or group, it has failed its purpose regardless of analytical quality. Refusal is a professional duty.

## 15. Security boundaries
Producing targeting-enabling outputs; releasing data on vulnerable groups without protective review; ignoring harm potential in tasking; complying with harmful requests without escalation; enabling persecution through 'neutral' data presentation.

## 16. Expected output
Harm assessments, output screening records, refusal log, and escalation records.

## 17. Quality checklist
- [ ] Classification label applied to every artifact
- [ ] Sources are public-only and logged with access timestamps
- [ ] Third-party tools verified against vetting checklist
- [ ] Chain-of-custody or handling record complete
- [ ] Audit entry written for every material action
- [ ] Peer or reviewer sign-off recorded where required
- [ ] Harm assessment at tasking on file
- [ ] Output harm screening completed
- [ ] Refusals documented with authority
