# Public Administration Analysis

State-domain intelligence file for the ANBU analytical repository. Focus: **public administration analysis** under Indonesia's constitutional order (UUD 1945) and its democratic institutions.

## Purpose

Analyze Indonesia's public administration system: service delivery, e-government, decentralization, and administrative reform. This file supports analysts examining government effectiveness, citizen satisfaction, and administrative modernization.

## Scope

Covers: public service delivery (pelayanan publik); e-government (e-gov); 'mal pelayanan publik' (public service malls); 'one-stop service' (OSS); decentralization impact on services; administrative reform under 'reformasi birokrasi'; 'Layanan Administrasi' (administrative services); 'perizinan' (licensing) system.

## Intelligence objectives

Enable analysts to (1) assess service delivery quality, (2) track e-government progress, (3) evaluate decentralization impact, (4) identify administrative bottlenecks, and (5) recommend improvements.

## Core concepts

Public administration = 'administrasi publik'; service delivery = 'pelayanan'; e-government = 'pemerintahan elektronik'; 'mal pelayanan publik' = public service mall; 'OSS' = one-stop service; 'perizinan' = licensing; 'desentralisasi' = decentralization.

## Analytical methodology

Use 'Laporan Kinerja' of ministries; analyze 'mal pelayanan publik' performance; track e-government initiatives; examine 'OSS' implementation; monitor 'reformasi birokrasi' reports.

## Step-by-step workflow

Step 1: map service delivery system. Step 2: assess e-government progress. Step 3: evaluate decentralization impact. Step 4: identify bottlenecks. Step 5: recommend improvements.

## Data requirements

Ministry performance reports; 'mal pelayanan publik' data; e-government portals; 'OSS' statistics; 'reformasi birokrasi' reports; citizen satisfaction surveys.

## Approved public sources

Each ministry's 'pelayanan' page; 'mal pelayanan publik' sites; e-government portals (e.g., 'Layanan Rakyat'); perpres.go.id; bpk.go.id. Verify service data via official reports, not anecdotes.

## Source verification standards

Cross-check service statistics with ministry reports; verify e-government claims with portal data; confirm 'OSS' implementation with local government reports.

## Analytical framework

Service delivery model: access → process → quality → satisfaction → reform. Identify weak links.

## Evidence requirements

Ministry performance reports, 'mal pelayanan publik' data, e-government metrics, 'OSS' statistics.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH for official performance data. MODERATE for citizen satisfaction assessments. LOW for anecdotal service complaints.

## Common analytical mistakes

Assuming e-government equals better service; ignoring regional variation; treating 'mal pelayanan publik' as universal solution; missing informal 'jasa' (fixers).

## Ethical considerations

Public service data is public. Avoid defamation when discussing service failures; stick to official findings.

## Security boundaries

Do not fabricate service statistics or invent 'corruption in services' without evidence.

## Expected output

Public administration memos with service assessment, e-government progress, and reform recommendations. Cite official data.

## Quality checklist

Service data verified? E-government progress tracked? Decentralization impact assessed? Bottlenecks identified? Recommendations evidence-based?
