# Constitution Analysis

State-domain intelligence file for the ANBU analytical repository. Focus: **constitution analysis** under Indonesia's constitutional order (UUD 1945) and its democratic institutions.

## Purpose

Map the constitutional architecture of the Republic of Indonesia as the supreme legal frame for all state-domain intelligence. UUD 1945 (1945 Constitution, amended 1999-2002) defines sovereignty, separation of powers, and fundamental rights that every ministry, agency, and regional government operates within. This file supports analysts who must determine whether a government action is constitutionally valid, ultra vires, or subject to judicial review.

## Scope

Covers: Preamble and articles of UUD 1945 (especially Pasal 1-3 sovereignty, Pasal 27-34 rights, Pasal 20-22 legislative, Pasal 24 judiciary, Pasal 33 economic democracy, Pasal 34 social welfare); the four amendment packages; MK (Mahkamah Konstitusi) jurisprudence on constitutional interpretation; MPR's limited role in amending the constitution; and the relationship between UUD 1945 and other laws (UU, PP, Perpres). Does NOT cover foreign constitutions, provincial regulations, or detailed case law unless directly bearing on constitutional questions.

## Intelligence objectives

Enable analysts to (1) identify the constitutional basis of any state institution's authority, (2) assess whether proposed regulations or policies exceed constitutional limits, (3) trace how MK decisions reshape government practice, (4) evaluate claims of constitutional violations, and (5) predict how constitutional amendments affect state capacity.

## Core concepts

UUD 1945 as 'hukum dasar tertulis' (written fundamental law); popular sovereignty (kedaulatan rakyat) per Pasal 1; checks and balances among legislative (DPR/DPD/MPR), executive (President, Vice President, ministers), and judiciary (MK, MA, KY); constitutional review (pengujian undang-undang); judicial independence; social justice clauses (Pasal 33-34) that mandate state welfare functions.

## Analytical methodology

Read official MK decisions via mkri.id; cross-reference UU text against UUD articles; track MPR amendment schedules; monitor MK news releases; compare government policy claims with constitutional provisions; use legal databases (peraturan.go.id) for formal hierarchy.

## Step-by-step workflow

Step 1: identify the constitutional article at issue. Step 2: locate the implementing law or regulation. Step 3: check MK decisions on that article. Step 4: assess whether the law is still in force (in cassation). Step 5: document findings with article citations.

## Data requirements

UUD 1945 full text (1945, 1999-2002 amendments); MPR TAP MPR (Ketetapan MPR) on amendments; MK decision summaries; UU/PP/Perpres implementing provisions; academic commentary from Indonesian constitutional scholars (Jimly Asshiddiqie, Mahfud MD).

## Approved public sources

mkri.id (MK decisions); peraturan.go.id (legislative database); mpr.go.id (MPR decisions); bpk.go.id (constitutional context); academic databases (JSTOR, Garuda). Verify that any cited 'constitution' is actually UUD 1945 as amended, not the 1949/1950 constitutions.

## Source verification standards

Each constitutional claim must cite the exact article number and amendment date; MK decisions must be checked against official PDF, not secondary summaries; verify the law has not been repealed or amended since citation.

## Analytical framework

Use a three-tier test: (1) text of UUD 1945, (2) MK interpretation, (3) implementing legislation. If tiers conflict, MK jurisprudence controls unless amended.

## Evidence requirements

Quoted constitutional text, MK decision numbers, law article references, dates of amendments. Never paraphrase 'the constitution says' without citing the article.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH when citing UUD 1945 text or MK decisions directly. MODERATE when interpreting doctrine or comparing with foreign constitutions. LOW for predictions about future amendments.

## Common analytical mistakes

Assuming UUD 1945 is unchanged since 1945 (ignore amendments); treating TAP MPR as still binding when superseded; confusing MK's judicial review power with cassation; ignoring Pasal 33-34 social clauses; relying on non-official 'constitution' translations.

## Ethical considerations

Constitutional analysis is public law; no privacy concerns. However, avoid using constitutional arguments to delegitimize elected governments without evidence. Respect institutional legitimacy.

## Security boundaries

No classified content. Do not fabricate MK decisions or invent article numbers. Protect against hallucinated 'constitutional principles' not found in the text.

## Expected output

Constitutional memos citing specific articles, MK decisions, and implementing laws. Include a confidence rating and source list.

## Quality checklist

Article cited? MK decision verified? Amendment status checked? Conflict with implementing law noted? Indonesian language sources preferred.
