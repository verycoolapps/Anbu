# Public Service Analysis

State-domain intelligence file for the ANBU analytical repository. Focus: **public service analysis** under Indonesia's constitutional order (UUD 1945) and its democratic institutions.

## Purpose

Analyze Indonesia's public services: service delivery quality, accessibility, efficiency, and citizen satisfaction. This file supports analysts examining 'pelayanan publik' and 'mal pelayanan publik'.

## Scope

Covers: 'pelayanan publik' (public service); 'mal pelayanan publik' (public service malls); 'OSS' (one-stop service); 'perizinan' (licensing); 'layanan kesehatan' (health services); 'layanan pendidikan' (education); 'layanan administrasi' (administrative); 'e-government' (e-gov); 'citizen satisfaction' surveys.

## Intelligence objectives

Enable analysts to (1) assess service quality, (2) evaluate accessibility, (3) analyze efficiency, (4) measure satisfaction, and (5) recommend improvements.

## Core concepts

Pelayanan publik = public service; 'mal pelayanan publik' = service mall; 'OSS' = one-stop; 'perizinan' = licensing; 'e-government' = e-gov; 'kepuasan' = satisfaction; 'aksesibilitas' = accessibility; 'efisiensi' = efficiency.

## Analytical methodology

Use ministry service data; analyze 'mal pelayanan publik' performance; track 'OSS' implementation; examine 'perizinan' system; monitor 'e-government' portals; assess satisfaction surveys.

## Step-by-step workflow

Step 1: map service types. Step 2: assess quality. Step 3: evaluate accessibility. Step 4: analyze efficiency. Step 5: measure satisfaction.

## Data requirements

Ministry service data; 'mal pelayanan publik' reports; 'OSS' statistics; 'perizinan' data; 'e-government' metrics; satisfaction surveys.

## Approved public sources

Each ministry's 'pelayanan' page; 'mal pelayanan publik' sites; 'e-government' portals; bps.go.id. Verify service data via official sources.

## Source verification standards

Cross-check service data with ministry reports; verify 'OSS' with local government data; confirm satisfaction with official surveys.

## Analytical framework

Service model: access → process → quality → satisfaction → improvement. Identify weak links.

## Evidence requirements

Service data, mall reports, OSS statistics, licensing data, e-gov metrics, satisfaction surveys.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH for official ministry data. MODERATE for satisfaction assessments. LOW for anecdotal service complaints.

## Common analytical mistakes

Assuming e-gov equals better service; ignoring regional variation; treating 'mal pelayanan publik' as universal; missing informal 'jasa'; relying on media service complaints.

## Ethical considerations

Service data is public. Avoid defamation; stick to official evidence.

## Security boundaries

Do not fabricate service data or invent 'corrupt services' without official findings.

## Expected output

Service memos with quality/accessibility/efficiency/satisfaction assessment and improvement recommendations. Cite ministry/service data.

## Quality checklist

Services mapped? Quality assessed? Accessibility evaluated? Efficiency analyzed? Satisfaction measured?
