# International Dataset Research

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Covers collection and analysis of international datasets: UN, World Bank, SIPRI, IISS, and other multilateral and think-tank data.

## Scope

Datasets from international organizations, think tanks, and research institutions covering security, development, conflict, and governance.

## Intelligence objectives

- Retrieve standardized international indicators
- Compare countries and regions on common metrics
- Analyze conflict, security, and development trends
- Build cross-national analytical frameworks

## Core concepts

- International indicator standardization
- Conflict and security datasets
- Development and governance indices
- Arms transfer and military expenditure data
- Data quality and methodology variation across sources

## Analytical methodology

Identify relevant international datasets and their methodologies. Prefer official organization data. Download bulk data where available. Understand indicator definitions and coverage. Analyze trends and rankings. Cross-check with national sources. Document dataset versions and methodologies.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define analytical question
2. Identify relevant datasets
3. Acquire data and methodology docs
4. Analyze indicators and trends
5. Cross-check with national sources
6. Document with source citations

## Data requirements

Target countries/regions; indicators needed; time period; analytical framework.

## Approved public sources

- UN data portals (UNdata, UNSD)
- World Bank Open Data
- SIPRI databases
- IISS Military Balance
- Freedom House
- Transparency International
- ACLED
- UCDP

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

International datasets are treated as standardized but with varying methodologies. Rankings are analyzed with awareness of indicator construction. Conflict data is cross-checked with news and official sources. Methodology differences are documented.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Treating rankings as absolute measures
- Ignoring methodology differences
- Missing data coverage gaps
- Failing to cross-check with national sources

## Ethical considerations

Use publicly available international data. Respect license terms. Do not use data for targeting.

## Security boundaries

No use of classified intelligence data. No data for operational targeting.

## Expected output

International analysis report with indicator tables, trend analysis, and source citations.

- [ ] Methodologies reviewed
- [ ] Coverage assessed
- [ ] Cross-checks performed
