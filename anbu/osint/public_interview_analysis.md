# Public Interview Analysis

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Defines methods for analyzing public interviews: content, framing, credibility assessment, and cross-interview comparison.

## Scope

Analysis of published interviews with officials, experts, witnesses, and public figures across media formats.

## Intelligence objectives

- Analyze interview content and claims
- Assess speaker credibility and consistency
- Compare accounts across interviews
- Extract expert assessments and testimony

## Core concepts

- Interview format and context
- Credibility assessment
- Consistency across interviews
- Expert versus eyewitness testimony
- Media framing of interviews

## Analytical methodology

Collect interview transcripts or reliable transcripts from video. Analyze claims, consistency, and credibility. Compare with other interviews and sources. Assess expert credentials. Flag contradictions and evasions. Grade confidence in claims.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Collect interview content with attribution
2. Transcribe or verify transcripts
3. Analyze claims and consistency
4. Compare with other sources
5. Assess credibility
6. Write analysis with confidence grading

## Data requirements

Interview content; speaker identity; date; format; related claims to verify.

## Approved public sources

- Published interview transcripts
- Video interview platforms
- News outlet interview archives
- Expert interview collections
- Parliamentary committee evidence sessions

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Interviews are analyzed for claims, consistency, and credibility. Expert interviews are weighted by credentials and methodology. Eyewitness accounts are assessed for proximity and consistency. Contradictions are preserved as contested findings.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Treating interview claims as verified without corroboration
- Ignoring interview context and editing
- Missing consistency across interviews
- Failing to assess speaker credibility

## Ethical considerations

Analyze publicly available interviews. Do not contact interviewees deceptively. Do not use interview analysis for targeting.

## Security boundaries

No deceptive interview techniques. No publication of private interview content.

## Expected output

Interview analysis report with claims inventory, consistency assessment, credibility rating, and confidence grading.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Transcript verified
- [ ] Claims extracted
- [ ] Consistency checked
- [ ] Credibility assessed
