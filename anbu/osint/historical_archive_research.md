# Historical Archive Research

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Covers research in historical archives: national archives, presidential libraries, colonial records, and historical document collections.

## Scope

Historical records from national archives, libraries, museums, and digital historical collections.

## Intelligence objectives

- Retrieve historical documents and records
- Build historical context for current developments
- Trace institutional histories and decisions
- Access declassified and historical intelligence records

## Core concepts

- Archive versus library versus museum collections
- Declassification and access restrictions
- Historical document authentication
- Archive cataloging and finding aids
- Digital versus physical archive access

## Analytical methodology

Identify relevant archives and their catalogs. Search finding aids and digital collections. For physical archives, document access requirements. For digital, retrieve documents lawfully. Analyze documents in historical context. Cross-reference with other historical and contemporary sources.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define historical question
2. Identify relevant archives
3. Search catalogs and finding aids
4. Retrieve documents (digital or document access needs)
5. Analyze in historical context
6. Cross-reference with other sources

## Data requirements

Historical period; topics; archives to search; access constraints.

## Approved public sources

- National archives (US National Archives, UK National Archives, etc.)
- Presidential libraries
- Colonial and imperial archives
- Historical society collections
- Declassified document collections (NSA, CIA reading rooms)
- Digital historical collections

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Historical documents are primary sources for their time. Access restrictions are documented. Declassified documents are labeled with declassification date. Historical context is essential for interpretation; presentist readings are flagged.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Reading historical documents without context
- Ignoring access restrictions
- Treating declassified documents as current
- Missing archive cataloging gaps

## Ethical considerations

Respect archive access rules and copyright. Do not remove documents from archives. Do not publish sensitive personal information found in historical records.

## Security boundaries

No removal or damage to archive materials. No circumvention of archive access controls.

## Expected output

Historical research report with document citations, context analysis, and access documentation.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Archive citations complete
- [ ] Access restrictions documented
- [ ] Historical context provided
- [ ] Declassification status noted
