# Public Financial Database

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Covers collection and analysis of public financial databases: corporate filings, market data, banking data, and financial disclosures.

## Scope

Publicly available financial data from regulators, exchanges, companies, and financial databases.

## Intelligence objectives

- Analyze corporate financial health and ownership
- Track market movements and financial flows
- Investigate financial sanctions and watchlists
- Correlate financial data with other intelligence domains

## Core concepts

- Corporate filings and disclosures
- Market data and indices
- Banking and payment system data
- Financial sanctions lists
- Anti-money laundering data
- Ownership and beneficial ownership disclosure

## Analytical methodology

Identify relevant financial databases and regulators. Prefer official regulatory filings. Download bulk data where available. Analyze financial statements, ownership structures, and market data. Cross-check with registry data. Document data sources and dates.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define financial question
2. Identify data sources
3. Acquire filings and market data
4. Analyze financial health and ownership
5. Cross-check with registry and economic data
6. Document with source citations

## Data requirements

Target entities; financial metrics; time period; regulatory jurisdictions.

## Approved public sources

- Securities and exchange commissions
- Stock exchanges and market data providers
- Corporate investor relations pages
- Financial regulators and central banks
- OFAC and sanctions lists
- OpenCorporates and registry aggregators

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Financial data is treated as authoritative for reported figures. Ownership structures are traced through filings and registries. Sanctions lists are authoritative for listed entities. Market data is analyzed for trends, not predictions.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Trusting reported figures without verification
- Ignoring beneficial ownership gaps
- Treating market data as predictive
- Missing regulatory filing nuances

## Ethical considerations

Use publicly available financial data. Respect copyright and license terms. Do not use financial data for insider trading or market manipulation.

## Security boundaries

No use of non-public financial information. No assistance with sanctions evasion or money laundering.

## Expected output

Financial analysis report with filings, ownership structures, market data, and source citations.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Filings verified
- [ ] Ownership traced
- [ ] Sanctions checked
- [ ] Sources documented
