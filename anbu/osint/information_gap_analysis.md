# Information Gap Analysis

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Defines methods for identifying information gaps: what is missing, why, and how to address gaps in collection.

## Scope

All collection cycles, ensuring requirements are fully addressed or gaps are explicitly documented.

## Intelligence objectives

- Identify missing information relative to requirements
- Classify gap types: source, access, language, temporal
- Determine gap impact on analytical confidence
- Plan gap-filling collection cycles

## Core concepts

- Gap types: source absence, access restriction, language barrier, temporal limitation
- Gap impact assessment
- Gap-filling strategies
- Negative findings versus collection gaps

## Analytical methodology

Compare collected items against requirement success criteria. Identify missing elements. Classify gaps by type. Assess impact on analytical confidence. For each gap, determine if it is fillable (additional collection) or structural (information does not exist publicly). Document gap-filling attempts and results.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Review collection log against requirements
2. Identify missing elements
3. Classify gap types
4. Assess gap impact
5. Attempt gap-filling collection
6. Document results and remaining gaps

## Data requirements

Collection requirement; collection log; success criteria; analytical confidence assessment.

## Approved public sources

- Collection log
- Collection requirement
- Source discovery results
- Access constraint records

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Gaps are classified: fillable (can be addressed with more collection), structural (information not publicly available), or access-limited (requires resources beyond mandate). Gap impact is graded by effect on analytical confidence.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Treating collection gaps as negative findings
- Ignoring gap impact on confidence
- Failing to attempt gap-filling
- Not documenting why gaps cannot be filled

## Ethical considerations

Gap analysis must not propose unlawful methods to fill gaps.

## Security boundaries

No gap-filling through unlawful or unethical means.

## Expected output

Gap analysis report with gap inventory, classification, impact assessment, and gap-filling recommendations.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Gaps identified
- [ ] Types classified
- [ ] Impact assessed
- [ ] Filling attempted
