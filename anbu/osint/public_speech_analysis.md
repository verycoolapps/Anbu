# Public Speech Analysis

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Provides methodology for analyzing public speeches: content, rhetoric, framing, and policy signal detection.

## Scope

Analysis of speeches by officials, political leaders, and public figures, including prepared remarks, debates, and public addresses.

## Intelligence objectives

- Analyze speech content and policy signals
- Detect rhetorical patterns and framing
- Compare speeches across speakers and time
- Extract policy commitments and their evolution

## Core concepts

- Prepared versus extemporaneous remarks
- Rhetorical devices and framing
- Policy signal detection
- Speech corpus analysis
- Attribution and context verification

## Analytical methodology

Collect verbatim speech texts from official sources. Analyze content, structure, and rhetorical devices. Compare across speeches and speakers. Extract policy commitments and track their evolution. Assess context (occasion, audience, preceding events). Grade confidence in interpretation.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Collect speech texts with attribution
2. Analyze content and structure
3. Extract policy signals and commitments
4. Compare with other speeches
5. Assess context and occasion
6. Write analysis with confidence grading

## Data requirements

Speech texts; speaker attribution; dates; occasions; related policy context.

## Approved public sources

- Official government speech archives
- Parliamentary debate records
- UN and multilateral speech archives
- Political party websites
- Verified video and transcript services

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Speeches are analyzed for content, framing, and policy signals. Prepared remarks are distinguished from extemporaneous comments. Interpretation is graded by context clarity and corroboration with other sources.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Analyzing paraphrased reports instead of verbatim text
- Ignoring speech context and occasion
- Over-interpreting rhetoric as policy
- Missing multilingual speech sources

## Ethical considerations

Analyze publicly delivered speeches. Do not use speech analysis to target or harass speakers.

## Security boundaries

No use of private communications. No speech analysis for harassment or targeting.

## Expected output

Speech analysis report with content analysis, policy signals, comparative findings, and confidence grading.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Verbatim text used
- [ ] Attribution verified
- [ ] Context documented
- [ ] Confidence graded
