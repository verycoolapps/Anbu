# OSINT Collection Ethics

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Defines the ethical framework governing all ANBU collection: boundaries, principles, and decision-making for ethically complex collection.

## Scope

All OSINT collection activity, providing ethical guidelines that override collection objectives when in conflict.

## Intelligence objectives

- Establish clear ethical boundaries for collection
- Provide decision framework for ethically complex situations
- Ensure collection serves legitimate analytical purposes
- Protect privacy and prevent harm

## Core concepts

- Public interest test
- Privacy versus public figure distinction
- Proportionality principle
- Harm prevention
- Transparency and accountability

## Analytical methodology

Every collection task is evaluated against ethical criteria: public interest justification, privacy impact, proportionality, harm potential, and legality. Collection that fails ethical review is not performed regardless of analytical value. Ethical decisions are documented. Gray areas are escalated for review.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Assess collection task against ethical criteria
2. Determine public interest justification
3. Evaluate privacy impact
4. Check proportionality and harm potential
5. Verify legality
6. Document ethical decision
7. Escalate gray areas for review

## Data requirements

Collection task description; target subjects; intended use; potential impact assessment.

## Approved public sources

- ANBU ethical guidelines
- Legal requirements
- Professional intelligence ethics
- Privacy regulations
- Platform terms of service

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Ethical framework uses five tests: public interest, privacy, proportionality, harm, legality. Collection must pass all five. Private individuals are protected unless public interest is overwhelming and harm is minimal. Gray areas are escalated, not resolved unilaterally.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Treating public availability as ethical carte blanche
- Ignoring privacy impact on private individuals
- Failing to document ethical decisions
- Not escalating gray areas

## Ethical considerations

This document IS the ethics framework. It cannot be overridden by collection objectives. Violations are reportable.

## Security boundaries

No collection that violates this framework. No exceptions without documented escalation and approval.

## Expected output

Ethical assessment for each collection task with decision, rationale, and escalation notes where applicable.
