# Public Economic Database

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Covers collection and analysis of public economic databases: statistics, trade, development indicators, and economic surveys.

## Scope

National statistical offices, international economic databases, trade data, and development indicators.

## Intelligence objectives

- Retrieve economic indicators and statistics
- Analyze economic trends and patterns
- Correlate economic data with political and security developments
- Build economic context for strategic analysis

## Core concepts

- Economic indicators: GDP, inflation, employment, trade
- Statistical methodology and revision
- Purchasing power parity and exchange rates
- Economic sanctions and trade restrictions data
- Informal economy estimation challenges

## Analytical methodology

Identify relevant economic databases and indicators. Prefer official statistical sources. Understand methodology notes and revision policies. Download bulk data where available. Analyze trends and correlations. Cross-check with other sources. Document data versions and retrieval dates.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define economic question and indicators needed
2. Identify data sources
3. Acquire data (bulk preferred)
4. Analyze trends and correlations
5. Cross-check with other sources
6. Document with source citations

## Data requirements

Target countries/regions; economic indicators; time period; analytical question.

## Approved public sources

- National statistical offices
- World Bank Open Data
- IMF data portals
- UN Comtrade
- OECD statistics
- National central banks
- Regional development banks

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Economic data is treated as authoritative for what it measures. Methodology notes govern interpretation. Revisions are tracked. Correlations with other domains are flagged as associations, not causation.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Using data without understanding methodology
- Ignoring revision risks
- Treating correlations as causation
- Missing informal economy and data quality issues

## Ethical considerations

Use publicly available economic data. Respect license terms. Do not use economic data for targeting.

## Security boundaries

No use of non-public economic intelligence. No economic data for sanctions evasion assistance.

## Expected output

Economic analysis report with indicator tables, trend analysis, and source citations.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Data sources documented
- [ ] Methodology reviewed
- [ ] Versions recorded
- [ ] Correlations flagged
