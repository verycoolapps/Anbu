# Academic Research Collection

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Defines procedures for collecting academic literature, datasets, and research outputs relevant to intelligence questions.

## Scope

Journal articles, working papers, theses, conference proceedings, and research datasets from academic sources.

## Intelligence objectives

- Locate peer-reviewed and grey literature on technical and policy topics
- Assess research quality and institutional provenance
- Extract datasets and findings for corroboration
- Identify research communities and expert voices

## Core concepts

- Peer-reviewed versus preprint versus grey literature
- Citation networks and seminal works
- Institutional provenance and funding disclosure
- Reproducibility and data availability statements

## Analytical methodology

Search academic indexes (Google Scholar, Web of Science where accessible, Scopus, JSTOR, SSRN, arXiv, institutional repositories). Prefer open-access versions. Record DOI, venue, authors, and access date. Assess venue quality and methodology. Where datasets accompany papers, log their repositories.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Translate requirement into scholarly keywords
2. Search indexes and repositories
3. Filter by venue, date, and methodology
4. Retrieve full text via open access or institutional access
5. Record bibliographic metadata and access method
6. Flag key findings and datasets for analysis

## Data requirements

Topic keywords; relevant disciplines; time window; language; access entitlements.

## Approved public sources

- Google Scholar
- Web of Science, Scopus, JSTOR
- arXiv, SSRN, bioRxiv and preprint servers
- Institutional repositories and university libraries
- Open access aggregators (CORE, Unpaywall)
- Research data repositories

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Academic sources are weighted by venue quality, methodology rigor, and reproducibility. Preprints are labeled as unreviewed. Citation counts inform impact but not truth. Research funded by interested parties is flagged for potential bias.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Citing abstracts without reading full methodology
- Treating preprints as peer-reviewed
- Ignoring replication failures and retractions
- Missing non-English scholarship

## Ethical considerations

Use lawful access: open access, institutional subscriptions, author manuscripts. Do not use Sci-Hub or pirate repositories.

## Security boundaries

No use of illicit document-sharing services. Respect publisher terms and copyright.

## Expected output

Annotated bibliography with access links, quality ratings, extracted findings, and dataset pointers.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] DOI/venue recorded
- [ ] Access method lawful
- [ ] Methodology assessed
- [ ] Preprints labeled
