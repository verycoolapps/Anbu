# Public Press Release Collection

> ANBU OSINT Division 002 — Collection Tradecraft

## Purpose

Covers systematic collection of press releases and media statements from corporations, NGOs, international organizations, and public bodies.

## Scope

Press release wires, corporate newsrooms, NGO communications, multilateral organization press offices, and crisis communications.

## Intelligence objectives

- Track organizational communications on matters of public interest
- Detect coordinated messaging across organizations
- Archive releases that are later removed or revised
- Build a corpus for narrative and framing analysis

## Core concepts

- Press release as authored organizational narrative
- Wire services versus direct publication
- Crisis communication patterns
- Release timing as signaling

## Analytical methodology

Enumerate target organizations' newsrooms and wire presence. Monitor RSS feeds and newsrooms directly where available. Capture full release text with publication timestamp. Archive copies; releases are frequently removed. Classify by organization type, subject, and tone markers.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Build target organization list from requirement
2. Identify newsroom URLs, RSS feeds, and wire presence
3. Monitor and capture releases with timestamps
4. Archive snapshots
5. Classify and log
6. Flag revisions, removals, and coordinated timing patterns

## Data requirements

Target organizations; subject areas; period; languages; wire services in scope.

## Approved public sources

- Corporate newsrooms and investor relations pages
- NGO and civil society press pages
- UN, NATO, EU, and other multilateral press rooms
- Wire services (PR Newswire, GlobeNewswire, regional wires)
- Crisis and incident communications pages

## Source verification standards

Every source is evaluated for authority, proximity to the event or subject, currency, and internal consistency before its content is used analytically. Primary sources are preferred over secondary accounts. Where sources conflict, the conflict is preserved and reported rather than silently resolved. Source reliability is rated on the ANBU reliability scale and recorded in the collection log.

## Analytical framework

Press releases are treated as organizational self-description: evidence of what an organization chooses to say, not of underlying truth. Framing analysis compares wording across organizations on the same event. Timing clusters may indicate coordination.

## Evidence requirements

Every analytical claim must be traceable to a logged collection item. Evidence requirements: (1) verbatim quotation or precise paraphrase with page/section reference; (2) source attribution including URL or archive identifier; (3) collection timestamp; (4) access method; (5) confidence grade per the ANBU scale. Claims without logged evidence are marked INSUFFICIENT and excluded from analytical products.

## Confidence assessment

HIGH: multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, no material contradictions. MODERATE: credible sources with minor gaps or single-source primary documentation. LOW: indirect, derivative, or partially corroborated reporting. INSUFFICIENT: evidence does not support a judgment; collection gap or contested sources prevent assessment.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Treating press releases as independent confirmation of each other
- Missing removed or revised releases
- Ignoring non-English releases
- Failing to capture metadata like embargo times

## Ethical considerations

Collect only publicly distributed releases. Do not subscribe to or access paid wire services through unauthorized means.

## Security boundaries

No credential sharing for paid wires, no scraping behind authentication.

## Expected output

Release corpus with organization attribution, timestamps, archive links, and classification tags.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Full text and timestamp captured
- [ ] Archive snapshot retained
- [ ] Organization type classified
- [ ] Removals/revisions flagged
