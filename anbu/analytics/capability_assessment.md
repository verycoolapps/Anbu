# Capability Assessment

**ANBU Division 008 · Analytics**

---

## Purpose

Capability assessment answers what an actor can do — independently of what it wants to do. Intent without capability is aspiration; capability without intent is potential. Together they bound the threat or opportunity space. This file defines how ANBU evaluates capabilities of states, militaries, institutions, and organizations from public evidence: what exists, what works, what is constrained, and what is likely to change.

## Scope

Applied to military capability (forces, equipment, logistics, training), state capacity (taxation, administration, service delivery), institutional capability (regulatory, judicial, technical), and organizational capability (NGOs, parties, corporations). Focuses on demonstrable capability, not aspirational claims.

## Intelligence objectives

- Inventory what capabilities exist and their demonstrated performance.
- Assess capability quality: readiness, sustainability, interoperability, resilience.
- Identify capability gaps and constraints: material, human, doctrinal, logistical.
- Track capability trends: modernization, atrophy, asymmetric development.

## Core concepts

- **Capability vs. capacity.** Capacity is potential (resources, manpower); capability is demonstrated ability to produce effects. A military with equipment but no training has capacity, not capability.
- **Demonstrated vs. theoretical capability.** What has been shown to work (exercises, operations, audits) vs. what should work on paper.
- **Readiness.** The state of being able to execute now — personnel, equipment, training, logistics, command.
- **Sustainability.** Ability to maintain capability over time — logistics, budgets, industrial base, personnel pipeline.
- **Asymmetric capability.** Capabilities that punch above weight: cyber, drones, proxies, information operations, economic leverage.
- **Capability trajectory.** Direction of change: modernization vs. decay, expansion vs. contraction.
- **Constraint analysis.** What limits capability: sanctions, geography, industrial capacity, human capital, doctrine.

## Analytical methodology

1. Define the capability question: what must the actor be able to do?
2. Inventory relevant assets: personnel, equipment, budgets, infrastructure, doctrine.
3. Assess demonstrated performance: exercises, operations, audits, outputs.
4. Evaluate readiness and sustainability separately.
5. Identify gaps and constraints.
6. Assess trajectory: is capability growing, stable, or declining?
7. Compare with intent via `strategic_intent_assessment.md` to bound the threat space.
8. Report capability statement with evidence per claim and confidence.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define the actor and the capability domain(s) in question.
2. Collect inventory data: force sizes, equipment lists, budgets, personnel numbers.
3. Collect performance data: exercise reports, operational history, audit results, service outputs.
4. Assess readiness: training levels, equipment serviceability, logistics, command coherence.
5. Assess sustainability: budget trends, recruitment, industrial capacity, doctrine.
6. Identify constraints: sanctions, geography, industrial limits, human capital gaps.
7. Evaluate trajectory: modernization programs, procurement cycles, capability decay indicators.
8. Write capability assessment: what exists, what works, what's constrained, what's changing.

## Data requirements

- Official force/personnel statistics and budget documents.
- Procurement announcements and contract awards.
- Exercise schedules and observer reports.
- Operational history and after-action assessments where public.
- Audit reports and inspector-general findings.
- Industrial capacity data: defense industry output, R&D spending.
- Training and readiness indicators where published.
- Service delivery statistics for state-capacity assessments.

## Approved public sources

Defense white papers and doctrine publications; budget and appropriations documents; procurement portals; official force statistics; exercise announcements and OSCE/UN observer reports; audit institution reports; SIPRI and IISS databases; national statistics offices; service delivery data; corporate filings for defense industry.

## Source verification standards

Capability claims require evidence of existence and performance. Inventory numbers from official sources; performance claims from demonstrated exercises or operations; aspirational programs flagged as programs, not capabilities. See `../verification/institutional_source_verification.md` and `../military/` standards.

## Analytical framework

Capability assessment dimensions:

| Dimension | Questions | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory | What exists? | Official statistics, procurement |
| Performance | What works? | Exercises, operations, audits |
| Readiness | Can it execute now? | Training, serviceability, logistics |
| Sustainability | Can it persist? | Budget, recruitment, industry |
| Constraints | What limits it? | Sanctions, geography, doctrine |
| Trajectory | Which direction? | Modernization, decay indicators |

## Evidence requirements

- Inventory claims cite official statistics or procurement records.
- Performance claims cite demonstrated exercises or operations.
- Readiness assessments cite observable indicators, not assumptions.
- Trajectory claims cite budget/procurement trends.
- Constraints evidenced, not assumed.

## Confidence assessment

- **HIGH** — comprehensive inventory + demonstrated performance + clear trajectory.
- **MODERATE** — inventory solid; performance partially demonstrated; trajectory emerging.
- **LOW** — inventory incomplete; performance mostly theoretical; trajectory unclear.
- **INSUFFICIENT** — opaque capability with few public indicators; report observability gap.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Counting hardware without assessing readiness or sustainability.
- Treating announced programs as existing capability.
- Ignoring demonstrated performance — paper capability ≠ real capability.
- Missing asymmetric capabilities because they don't fit conventional metrics.
- Assuming capability equals intent (capability without intent is latent).
- Ignoring decay: capabilities atrophy without sustained investment.
- Comparing nominal numbers without adjusting for quality, training, or context.

## Ethical considerations

Capability assessment of military and state institutions is legitimate security analysis. It must not be used to target individuals or to advocate for offensive action. Assessments must distinguish capability from intent to avoid threat inflation.

## Security boundaries

Public sources only. No claims about classified capabilities, secret programs, or actual operational readiness beyond public evidence. No capability assessment framed as targeting or attack planning.

## Expected output

Capability annex: inventory summary, performance assessment, readiness/sustainability evaluation, constraints, trajectory, confidence. Main product integrates capability with intent to bound the threat or opportunity space.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Inventory from official/procurement sources.
- [ ] Performance based on demonstrated evidence.
- [ ] Readiness and sustainability assessed separately.
- [ ] Constraints identified and evidenced.
- [ ] Trajectory direction stated with evidence.
- [ ] Capability vs. intent distinction maintained.
