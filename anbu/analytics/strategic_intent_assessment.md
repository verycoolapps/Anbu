# Strategic Intent Assessment

**ANBU Division 008 · Analytics**

---

## Purpose

Strategic intent assessment answers the hardest question in intelligence: what does the actor actually want, and how committed are they to getting it? Public statements announce intentions; behavior reveals them. This file defines how ANBU assesses strategic intent from public evidence — distinguishing declared objectives, inferred objectives, and revealed preferences — while resisting the twin failures of taking rhetoric at face value and ignoring it entirely.

## Scope

Applied to state actors (governments, militaries, ruling parties), international organizations, major political movements, and institutional actors whose strategic direction matters. Not applied to individuals' private intentions.

## Intelligence objectives

- Distinguish declared intent (what actors say) from assessed intent (what evidence supports).
- Evaluate commitment: how much will the actor invest, risk, and persist?
- Identify intent shifts: when declared and revealed intent diverge or change.
- Provide the intent layer for capability assessment and warning products.

## Core concepts

- **Declared intent.** Official statements, doctrine, strategy documents, campaign promises. Cheap to produce, often strategically misleading.
- **Assessed intent.** The analyst's judgment of what the actor actually seeks, based on behavior, resource allocation, and pattern analysis.
- **Revealed preference.** What actors do when rhetoric and action diverge — budgets, deployments, appointments, negotiations.
- **Commitment gradient.** Intent ranges from aspirational (cheap talk) to vital (existential). Assess where on the gradient the objective sits.
- **Intent vs. capability.** What an actor wants vs. what it can do; both are needed for threat assessment, neither substitutes for the other.
- **Strategic culture.** Historical patterns of how the actor uses force, negotiates, and defines security — context for interpreting intent.
- **Audience costs.** Public commitments constrain future behavior; domestic and international audiences raise the cost of reversal.

## Analytical methodology

1. Collect declared intent: official statements, doctrine, strategy documents, legislative programs.
2. Collect revealed preference: budgets, deployments, appointments, negotiation behavior, alliance choices.
3. Identify divergences: where action contradicts rhetoric.
4. Assess commitment: resource investment, risk acceptance, persistence through setbacks.
5. Check strategic culture: does the behavior fit historical patterns?
6. Evaluate intent stability: is the objective enduring or tactical?
7. Formulate assessed intent with confidence and the evidence that would change it.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. Define the actor and the strategic question (what do they want regarding X?).
2. Assemble declared intent: latest official statements, doctrine, white papers, legislative agendas.
3. Assemble revealed preference: budget allocations, force posture, diplomatic moves, domestic political investments.
4. Build the divergence table: declared vs. revealed on each objective.
5. Assess commitment level per objective: rhetorical / material / vital.
6. Cross-check with strategic culture and historical behavior patterns.
7. Test against `analysis_of_competing_hypotheses.md`: could the behavior support alternative intent readings?
8. Write assessed intent: objectives ranked by commitment, evidence per assessment, confidence.

## Data requirements

- Official statements, doctrine, strategy documents (dated).
- Budget and resource allocation data showing investment priorities.
- Deployment, exercise, and procurement patterns.
- Diplomatic behavior: negotiations, alliances, sanctions responses.
- Leadership statements across time for consistency analysis.
- Historical behavior for strategic-culture baseline.
- Domestic political context affecting stated vs. real intent.

## Approved public sources

Official statements and readouts; defense white papers and doctrine publications; budget documents and appropriations; exercise announcements and observation; diplomatic communiqués; parliamentary debates; leadership speeches; academic and think-tank strategic analysis; declassified historical documents.

## Source verification standards

Declared intent comes from primary official sources. Inferred intent must cite the behavioral evidence supporting it. Single-source claims of secret intent are inadmissible; assessments must rest on observable behavior. See `../verification/elite_source_verification.md`.

## Analytical framework

Intent assessment matrix:

| Objective | Declared | Revealed | Commitment | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [objective 1] | Strong rhetoric | Material investment | High | Multi-source |
| [objective 2] | Routine mention | No resource shift | Low | Single source |

Divergence interpretation guide:

- Rhetoric high, resources low → aspirational or domestic-posturing intent.
- Rhetoric low, resources high → concealed or denied intent; investigate carefully.
- Both high → committed intent; assess capability and timing via `capability_assessment.md`.

## Evidence requirements

- Declared intent cited to specific dated statements.
- Assessed intent cites behavioral evidence, not just absence of contradiction.
- Divergences disclosed explicitly.
- Commitment assessments cite resource investment or risk acceptance.
- Confidence reflects evidence breadth and divergence resolution.

## Confidence assessment

- **HIGH** — declared and revealed intent align across multiple objectives; commitment evidenced by sustained resource investment.
- **MODERATE** — main objectives identifiable; some divergence unresolved or commitment ambiguous.
- **LOW** — significant divergence between rhetoric and behavior; intent uncertain.
- **INSUFFICIENT** — insufficient public evidence to assess intent beyond declared positions; report gap.

## Common analytical mistakes

- Taking official statements as true intent without behavioral corroboration.
- Ignoring revealed preference — budgets and deployments speak louder than speeches.
- Mirror-imaging: assuming the actor wants what we would want in their position.
- Confusing tactical signals with strategic intent.
- Ignoring domestic political incentives that produce insincere declarations.
- Treating intent as fixed; it evolves with circumstances.
- Assuming concealment equals hostile intent; sometimes it equals routine discretion.

## Ethical considerations

Intent assessment must be based on public behavior and stated policy, not on profiling or speculation about private psychology. Assessments must not be framed as predicting individual leaders' private desires; they assess actor behavior patterns.

## Security boundaries

Public statements and observable behavior only. No claims about private deliberations, secret orders, or classified intentions. No intent assessment used to justify targeting or pre-emptive action against lawful actors.

## Expected output

Intent annex: declared vs. assessed intent table, commitment ranking, divergence analysis, evidence per assessment, confidence. Main product integrates intent into the strategic assessment or warning product.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Declared and revealed intent both assembled.
- [ ] Divergences identified and interpreted.
- [ ] Commitment assessed via resource investment, not rhetoric.
- [ ] Strategic culture checked.
- [ ] Alternative intent readings tested.
- [ ] Confidence reflects evidence quality and divergence resolution.
