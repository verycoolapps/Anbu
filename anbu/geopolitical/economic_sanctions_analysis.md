# Economic Sanctions Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze economic sanctions — their design, implementation, effectiveness, evasion, and humanitarian impact — as tools of statecraft and instruments of international pressure.
## 2. Scope

- Sanctions regimes: UN, unilateral, and multilateral — financial, trade, arms, and targeted sanctions. Geoeconomic strategy broadly is covered under geoeconomic_analysis.md.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Catalog active sanctions regimes and their design features.
- Assess implementation: enforcement, evasion, and compliance.
- Evaluate effectiveness: did sanctions achieve stated objectives?
- Analyze humanitarian impact and unintended consequences.
## 4. Core concepts

- Sanctions design and scope
- Enforcement and evasion
- Effectiveness measurement
- Humanitarian impact
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Sanctions-analysis framework: map regime design, assess implementation and evasion, evaluate effectiveness against stated objectives, and analyze humanitarian and second-order effects.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the sanctions regime or target state.
- Extract design features: scope, exemptions, enforcement mechanisms.
- Assess implementation: compliance rates, evasion methods, enforcement capacity.
- Evaluate effectiveness: stated objectives vs observed outcomes.
- Analyze humanitarian impact: GDP, trade, humanitarian exemptions.
- Review evasion networks and third-party effects.
## 7. Data requirements

- UN sanctions committee reports
- OFAC, EU, and national sanctions lists
- World Bank and IMF economic data
- UN Comtrade trade data
- Think-tank sanctions studies (CSIS, RAND)
- Academic sanctions literature
## 8. Approved public sources

- UN
- OFAC
- EU sanctions bodies
- World Bank
- IMF
- UN Comtrade
- CSIS
- RAND
## 9. Source verification standards

- Sanctions effectiveness is contested; present multiple assessments. Distinguish design flaws from
implementation failures. Note that sanctions data often lags; use latest available.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Sanctions-analysis framework: map regime design, assess implementation and evasion, evaluate effectiveness against stated objectives, and analyze humanitarian and second-order effects.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming sanctions are cost-free for the sender.
- Treating evasion as binary rather than adaptive.
- Ignoring humanitarian exemptions and their abuse.
- Measuring effectiveness only by target behavior change.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in sanctions evasion or circumvention.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate sanctions evasion, money laundering, or circumvention of export
controls.
## 16. Expected output

- A sanctions-analysis brief: regime design, implementation assessment, effectiveness evaluation, humanitarian
impact, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Regime defined
- Design features extracted
- Implementation assessed
- Effectiveness evaluated
- Humanitarian impact analyzed
- Confidence graded
