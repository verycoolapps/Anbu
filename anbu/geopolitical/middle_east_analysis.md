# Middle East Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze Middle East security: state rivalry, proxy dynamics, sectarian tensions, great-power involvement, and the evolving regional order.
## 2. Scope

- Middle East security dynamics: Israel–Iran rivalry, Gulf state competition, proxy conflicts, sectarian tensions, and external power involvement. North Africa is covered separately where relevant.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map Middle East security dynamics and key rivalries.
- Analyze proxy conflicts and their drivers.
- Assess great-power involvement and regional order.
- Evaluate sectarian tensions and their security effects.
## 4. Core concepts

- State rivalry
- Proxy warfare
- Sectarian tensions
- Great-power involvement
- Regional order
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Middle-East-framework: map rivalries, analyze proxy dynamics, assess great-power involvement, evaluate sectarian tensions, and forecast regional security trajectories.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the Middle East scope.
- Map key rivalries: Israel–Iran, Saudi–Iran, Turkey–Gulf states, etc.
- Analyze proxy conflicts: Syria, Yemen, Iraq, Lebanon.
- Assess great-power involvement: US, Russia, China roles.
- Evaluate sectarian tensions: Sunni-Shia, Kurdish issues, minority rights.
- Analyze regional order: Abraham Accords, Gulf cooperation, Iran's position.
## 7. Data requirements

- SIPRI and IISS assessments
- CSIS and RAND Middle East studies
- Official MFA statements of regional states
- UN reports and resolutions
- Think-tank Middle East analyses
- News reporting on regional conflicts
## 8. Approved public sources

- SIPRI
- IISS
- CSIS
- RAND
- UN
- National MFAs
- Think tanks (MEI, WINEP)
## 9. Source verification standards

- Middle East is not monolithic; avoid sectarian framing that essentializes identity. Note that proxy dynamics
are often overstated; many conflicts have local drivers. Distinguish state interests from non-state actors.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Middle-East-framework: map rivalries, analyze proxy dynamics, assess great-power involvement, evaluate sectarian tensions, and forecast regional security trajectories.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating Middle East as a single conflict zone.
- Overstating sectarian drivers vs political/economic ones.
- Ignoring local agency and autonomy.
- Assuming external powers control regional dynamics.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in regional conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on regional actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- A Middle East brief: rivalry map, proxy analysis, great-power assessment, sectarian review, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Rivalries mapped
- Proxy conflicts analyzed
- Great-power involvement assessed
- Sectarian tensions reviewed
- Confidence graded
