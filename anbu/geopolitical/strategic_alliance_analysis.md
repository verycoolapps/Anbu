# Strategic Alliance Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze alliance formation, cohesion, burden-sharing, and credibility — formal treaties and informal security partnerships alike — to assess reliability and strategic effect.
## 2. Scope

- Security alliances and strategic partnerships between states: NATO, bilateral alliances, ad-hoc coalitions, and alignment structures short of formal treaty.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map alliance membership, obligations, and decision procedures.
- Assess cohesion: shared threat perception, burden-sharing, interoperability.
- Evaluate credibility: commitment mechanisms, extended deterrence, abandonment vs entrapment risks.
- Forecast alliance behavior under stress scenarios.
## 4. Core concepts

- Collective defense vs collective security
- Abandonment and entrapment dilemmas
- Burden-sharing and free-riding
- Alliance cohesion under stress
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Alliance-lifecycle framework: reconstruct formation drivers, map obligations and decision rules, assess cohesion indicators, evaluate credibility mechanisms, and stress-test against plausible scenarios.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the alliance or partnership and its legal basis.
- Extract obligations: Article 5-type clauses, basing rights, command arrangements.
- Assess cohesion: defense spending shares, exercise participation, procurement alignment.
- Evaluate credibility: nuclear umbrella, troop presence, political will signals.
- Stress-test: how does the alliance behave under limited-war, gray-zone, or abandonment scenarios?
- Grade cohesion and credibility; forecast behavior.
## 7. Data requirements

- Alliance treaties and communiqués
- NATO and partnership official documents
- National defense white papers
- Exercise and deployment records
- SIPRI and IISS assessments
- Think-tank alliance studies
## 8. Approved public sources

- NATO
- Alliance secretariats
- SIPRI
- IISS
- CSIS
- RAND
- Official defense ministries
## 9. Source verification standards

- Alliance statements are cheap talk; weigh capability and past behavior. Distinguish formal obligations from
political commitments. Note that alliances can deter through ambiguity as well as clarity.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Alliance-lifecycle framework: reconstruct formation drivers, map obligations and decision rules, assess cohesion indicators, evaluate credibility mechanisms, and stress-test against plausible scenarios.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming treaty text equals operational reality.
- Ignoring divergent threat perceptions among members.
- Overstating cohesion during peacetime readiness exercises.
- Forgetting that alliances can entrap members into unwanted wars.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no advice on exploiting alliance weaknesses operationally.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. No assistance in alliance sabotage, covert pressure, or intelligence sharing violations.
## 16. Expected output

- An alliance dossier: obligations map, cohesion grade, credibility assessment, stress-test results, behavior
forecast.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Alliance defined
- Obligations mapped
- Cohesion indicators cited
- Credibility mechanisms assessed
- Stress tests described
- Confidence graded
