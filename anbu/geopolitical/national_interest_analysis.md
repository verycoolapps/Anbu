# National Interest Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Reconstruct a state's declared, revealed, and inferred national interests from public evidence, providing the analytical baseline for bilateral, alliance, and conflict assessment.
## 2. Scope

- A single state's external-facing priorities: territorial integrity, economic security, regime security, ideological aims, and alliance obligations. Internal policy detail is out of scope unless it constrains foreign policy.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Enumerate a state's declared strategic priorities from official doctrine and statements.
- Distinguish revealed interests (behavior) from declared interests (rhetoric).
- Identify which interests are vital, important, or peripheral — and where they conflict.
- Provide a ranked interest hierarchy usable by downstream analyses.
## 4. Core concepts

- Declared vs revealed preference
- Vital vs peripheral interests
- Interest aggregation and trade-offs
- Strategic culture
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Interest-hierarchy framework: collect official statements, budget allocations, force posture, treaty commitments, and observed behavior; classify each interest by centrality and consistency; infer the underlying hierarchy.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Identify the target state and timeframe.
- Collect official doctrine: defense white papers, national security strategies, MFA statements.
- Extract revealed preferences: budget lines, procurement programs, treaty commitments, voting patterns.
- Compare declared vs revealed; flag gaps as either deception, aspiration, or constraint.
- Rank interests by centrality and construct the hierarchy.
- Grade the assessment and note where evidence is thin.
## 7. Data requirements

- National security strategies and white papers
- Defense budgets and procurement records
- Treaty and alliance commitments
- UN voting records
- Official MFA and head-of-state statements
- Academic and think-tank country studies
## 8. Approved public sources

- UN
- National defense ministries
- Ministries of foreign affairs
- SIPRI
- World Bank
- CSIS
- RAND
- IISS
## 9. Source verification standards

- Primary documents outrank secondary commentary. Verify quotes against official transcripts. Treat rhetoric
during election cycles as lower-weight evidence.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Interest-hierarchy framework: collect official statements, budget allocations, force posture, treaty commitments, and observed behavior; classify each interest by centrality and consistency; infer the underlying hierarchy.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Confusing what leaders say with what the state does.
- Attributing monolithic intent to coalitions or bureaucracies.
- Ignoring domestic constraints (economy, public opinion, factional politics) that shape revealed preferences.
- Over-reading a single speech or incident as a doctrine shift.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only. Do not construct profiles usable for targeting individuals or coercing officials.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. No intelligence collection against persons; no facilitation of influence operations.
## 16. Expected output

- A ranked national-interest hierarchy with each entry labeled FACT/ASSESSMENT/INFERENCE and a confidence grade.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Declared and revealed interests separated
- Hierarchy ranked with justification
- Evidence labels applied per claim
- Domestic constraints noted
- Competing interpretations preserved
- Confidence graded
