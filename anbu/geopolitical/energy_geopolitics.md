# Energy Geopolitics Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze the geopolitical dimensions of energy: resource control, supply routes, chokepoints, price mechanisms, and the energy transition's strategic effects.
## 2. Scope

- Global and regional energy systems: oil, gas, coal, nuclear, and renewables — their geography, trade flows, chokepoints, and strategic leverage. Energy economics detail is covered under geoeconomics.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map global energy production, reserves, and consumption geography.
- Analyze critical energy chokepoints and transit routes.
- Assess energy leverage: who can disrupt, who depends, who profits.
- Evaluate the energy transition's geopolitical effects.
## 4. Core concepts

- Resource curse and rentier states
- Energy chokepoints and transit states
- Energy leverage and weaponization
- Energy transition disruption
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Energy-geopolitics framework: map reserves/production/consumption, trace trade flows and chokepoints, assess leverage asymmetries, and evaluate transition effects on power relations.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the energy scope (global, regional, or commodity-specific).
- Collect production/reserve/consumption data from authoritative sources.
- Map trade routes, pipelines, and maritime chokepoints.
- Assess leverage: dependence ratios, substitution options, strategic reserves.
- Analyze weaponization history and capability.
- Evaluate transition effects: critical minerals, stranded assets, new dependencies.
## 7. Data requirements

- IEA and EIA data
- BP Statistical Review and energy outlooks
- OPEC and national energy statistics
- UN Comtrade energy trade data
- Think-tank energy studies (IISS, CSIS)
- Official energy ministry publications
## 8. Approved public sources

- IEA
- EIA
- OPEC
- UN Comtrade
- World Bank
- IISS
- CSIS
- National energy ministries
## 9. Source verification standards

- Reserves ≠ production ≠ consumption; do not conflate. Note that energy data has significant revisions; use
latest available and note vintage. Distinguish proven reserves from technically recoverable.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Energy-geopolitics framework: map reserves/production/consumption, trace trade flows and chokepoints, assess leverage asymmetries, and evaluate transition effects on power relations.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating reserves as immediate power without production capacity.
- Ignoring substitution and efficiency responses to price shocks.
- Assuming chokepoints are permanently vulnerable rather than adaptable.
- Missing the energy transition's demand-destruction effects.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in energy sanctions evasion or infrastructure sabotage.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on energy infrastructure or sanctions circumvention.
## 16. Expected output

- An energy-geopolitics brief: resource map, chokepoint analysis, leverage assessment, transition implications,
confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Production/consumption mapped
- Chokepoints identified
- Leverage asymmetries assessed
- Transition effects analyzed
- Confidence graded
