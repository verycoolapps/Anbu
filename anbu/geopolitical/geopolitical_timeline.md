# Geopolitical Timeline Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Construct and analyze timelines of diplomatic, military, and economic events to identify turning points, escalation rhythms, and cyclical patterns in international affairs.
## 2. Scope

- Event sequences at strategic scale: crises, summits, sanctions, conflicts, and institutional milestones. Single-event analysis is covered elsewhere.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Build a verified chronological spine of events for a defined issue or region.
- Identify turning points and regime shifts in the sequence.
- Detect cyclical patterns: escalation rhythms, negotiation windows, seasonal drivers.
- Use the timeline to test causal hypotheses about outcomes.
## 4. Core concepts

- Event sequencing
- Turning-point identification
- Escalation rhythm
- Cyclical vs linear change
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Timeline-construction framework: collect dated events from reliable sources, verify each entry, sequence them, mark turning points, and analyze rhythm and pattern for forecasting.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the issue, region, and time window.
- Collect dated events from primary and credible secondary sources.
- Verify dates and facts; discard or flag unverifiable entries.
- Sequence events into a timeline; mark turning points.
- Analyze rhythm: escalation speed, negotiation windows, cyclical recurrence.
- Test hypotheses: does the pattern predict near-term events?
## 7. Data requirements

- Official chronologies (UN, MFA, defense ministries)
- Treaty and agreement signature dates
- News archives (Reuters, AP)
- Think-tank timelines and crisis chronologies
- Academic historical scholarship
- Declassified document collections
## 8. Approved public sources

- UN
- National MFAs
- Defense ministries
- Reuters/AP archives
- Think tanks (CSIS, RAND)
- Academic histories
## 9. Source verification standards

- Every timeline entry must be dated and sourced. Distinguish event date from report date. Mark disputed dates
explicitly rather than choosing silently.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Timeline-construction framework: collect dated events from reliable sources, verify each entry, sequence them, mark turning points, and analyze rhythm and pattern for forecasting.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Confusing correlation in time with causation.
- Ignoring events that did not happen (roads not taken).
- Overfitting patterns to small samples.
- Mixing verified dates with approximations without flagging.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no revisionism advocacy or falsification of historical record.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not fabricate events or alter historical records for narrative purposes.
## 16. Expected output

- A sourced timeline with turning-point analysis, rhythm assessment, and pattern-based forecast with confidence
labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Window defined
- Events dated and sourced
- Turning points marked
- Rhythm analyzed
- Hypotheses tested
- Confidence graded
