# International Trade Policy Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze international trade policy — tariffs, non-tariff barriers, preferential agreements, and trade disputes — for strategic objectives, economic effects, and geopolitical implications.
## 2. Scope

- Trade policy instruments and negotiations: tariffs, quotas, subsidies, rules of origin, and dispute settlement. Sanctions are covered under economic_sanctions_analysis.md.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Catalog trade policy instruments and their strategic uses.
- Analyze preferential trade agreements and their geopolitical effects.
- Assess trade disputes and their resolution mechanisms.
- Evaluate the strategic implications of trade policy choices.
## 4. Core concepts

- Trade policy instruments
- Preferential trade agreements
- Trade dispute settlement
- Strategic trade policy
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Trade-policy framework: catalog instruments, map agreement networks, assess dispute dynamics, and evaluate strategic implications for states and blocs.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the trade policy question or state pair.
- Catalog instruments: tariffs, quotas, subsidies, standards, rules of origin.
- Map preferential agreements: PTAs, FTAs, customs unions.
- Review trade disputes: WTO cases, bilateral disputes, retaliatory measures.
- Assess economic effects: trade diversion, welfare, competitiveness.
- Evaluate strategic implications: leverage, dependence, alliance effects.
## 7. Data requirements

- WTO dispute settlement records
- UN Comtrade and trade statistics
- Official trade ministry publications
- Preferential agreement texts
- Think-tank trade studies (PIIE, CSIS)
- Academic trade-policy literature
## 8. Approved public sources

- WTO
- UN Comtrade
- World Bank
- IMF
- PIIE
- CSIS
- National trade ministries
## 9. Source verification standards

- Trade data has reporting lags and valuation issues; note methodology. Distinguish bound vs applied tariffs.
Preferential agreements have complex rules; cite the agreement text.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Trade-policy framework: catalog instruments, map agreement networks, assess dispute dynamics, and evaluate strategic implications for states and blocs.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating tariffs as the only trade policy instrument.
- Ignoring non-tariff barriers and regulatory divergence.
- Assuming trade agreements are purely economic rather than geopolitical.
- Missing the role of subsidies and industrial policy.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in trade fraud or circumvention.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate customs fraud, transfer pricing abuse, or trade sanctions evasion.
## 16. Expected output

- A trade-policy brief: instrument catalog, agreement map, dispute analysis, strategic implications, confidence
labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Question defined
- Instruments cataloged
- Agreements mapped
- Disputes reviewed
- Effects assessed
- Confidence graded
