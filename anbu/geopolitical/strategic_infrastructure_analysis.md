# Strategic Infrastructure Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze strategic infrastructure — ports, bases, energy facilities, digital networks, and transport hubs — for geopolitical significance, vulnerability, and strategic competition.
## 2. Scope

- Physical and digital infrastructure with strategic value: ports, military bases, energy plants, undersea cables, and transport nodes. Generic infrastructure economics is out of scope.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Identify infrastructure assets with strategic significance.
- Assess their geopolitical role: power projection, economic leverage, surveillance.
- Map ownership, control, and access arrangements.
- Evaluate vulnerability and strategic competition over infrastructure.
## 4. Core concepts

- Dual-use infrastructure
- Infrastructure as leverage
- Strategic asset ownership
- Critical infrastructure protection
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Infrastructure-strategy framework: identify strategic assets, map ownership/control, assess geopolitical roles, evaluate vulnerabilities, and analyze competition over infrastructure.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the infrastructure category and region.
- Identify strategic assets: ports, bases, energy, cables, hubs.
- Map ownership and control: state, private, joint ventures.
- Assess geopolitical roles: military access, economic leverage, surveillance.
- Evaluate vulnerabilities: physical, cyber, political.
- Analyze competition: who wants access, who can deny it?
## 7. Data requirements

- Official infrastructure registries
- Port and base directories
- Energy facility databases
- Submarine cable maps (TeleGeography)
- Think-tank infrastructure studies (CSIS, RAND)
- Corporate disclosures
## 8. Approved public sources

- National infrastructure ministries
- Port authorities
- Energy regulators
- TeleGeography
- CSIS
- RAND
- Corporate filings
## 9. Source verification standards

- Ownership structures are often opaque (shell companies, joint ventures); mark uncertainty. Distinguish legal
ownership from effective control. Note that infrastructure significance is context-dependent.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Infrastructure-strategy framework: identify strategic assets, map ownership/control, assess geopolitical roles, evaluate vulnerabilities, and analyze competition over infrastructure.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating ownership as control without examining governance.
- Ignoring dual-use potential of civilian infrastructure.
- Assuming infrastructure is static rather than contested.
- Missing the cyber dimension of infrastructure vulnerability.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in infrastructure sabotage or espionage.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on infrastructure or corporate espionage.
## 16. Expected output

- An infrastructure-strategy brief: asset map, ownership analysis, geopolitical roles, vulnerability assessment,
competition analysis, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Category defined
- Assets identified
- Ownership mapped
- Roles assessed
- Vulnerabilities evaluated
- Confidence graded
