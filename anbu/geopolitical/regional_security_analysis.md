# Regional Security Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze regional security complexes — their threat perceptions, military balances, alliance structures, and conflict patterns — to assess stability and escalation risks.
## 2. Scope

- Regional security dynamics: threat perceptions, military balances, alliance structures, and conflict patterns within a defined region. Global power structure is covered separately.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map regional threat perceptions and their divergence.
- Assess the regional military balance and its trajectory.
- Analyze alliance structures and their cohesion.
- Evaluate conflict patterns: active, frozen, and latent disputes.
## 4. Core concepts

- Regional security complex
- Threat perception divergence
- Military balance
- Alliance cohesion
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Regional-security framework: map threat perceptions, assess military balances, analyze alliance structures, and evaluate conflict patterns and escalation risks.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the region and its security boundaries.
- Map threat perceptions: what does each state fear? From whom?
- Assess military balance: forces, posture, modernization, exercises.
- Analyze alliance structures: formal and informal security arrangements.
- Review conflict patterns: active disputes, frozen conflicts, latent tensions.
- Evaluate escalation risks and stability.
## 7. Data requirements

- SIPRI military expenditure and arms transfers
- IISS Military Balance
- Official defense ministry publications
- Regional organization security statements
- Think-tank regional studies (CSIS, IISS)
- News reporting on incidents
## 8. Approved public sources

- SIPRI
- IISS
- UN
- Regional organizations
- CSIS
- RAND
- National defense ministries
## 9. Source verification standards

- Threat perceptions are inferred from behavior and statements; label as assessments. Military balance is not
just hardware — doctrine, training, and geography matter. Note that regional dynamics are often driven by
external powers.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Regional-security framework: map threat perceptions, assess military balances, analyze alliance structures, and evaluate conflict patterns and escalation risks.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming shared threat perception within a region.
- Treating military balance as static rather than dynamic.
- Ignoring extra-regional powers' influence.
- Missing sub-state and non-state security actors.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in regional conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on regional actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- A regional-security brief: threat map, balance assessment, alliance analysis, conflict register, escalation
risks, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Region defined
- Threats mapped
- Balance assessed
- Alliances analyzed
- Conflicts reviewed
- Confidence graded
