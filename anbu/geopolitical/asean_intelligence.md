# ASEAN Intelligence Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze ASEAN as an institution and strategic actor: its decision-making, internal cohesion, external partnerships, and role in regional security and economic architecture.
## 2. Scope

- ASEAN's institutional performance, internal dynamics, and strategic role — covering consensus-building, centrality, external partnerships, and limitations. Southeast Asia broadly is covered in southeast_asia_analysis.md.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Analyze ASEAN's institutional design and decision-making.
- Assess internal cohesion and the consensus principle's effects.
- Evaluate ASEAN's external partnerships and centrality.
- Assess ASEAN's effectiveness in security and economic governance.
## 4. Core concepts

- ASEAN consensus and non-intervention
- Centrality and convening power
- External partnerships
- Institutional effectiveness
## 5. Analytical methodology

- ASEAN-framework: analyze institutional design, assess internal cohesion, evaluate external partnerships, and assess effectiveness in security and economic governance.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the ASEAN aspect under analysis.
- Extract institutional design: membership, decision rules, secretariat.
- Assess internal cohesion: member-state interests, divisions, consensus challenges.
- Evaluate external partnerships: dialogue partners, free trade agreements.
- Review security role: ASEAN Regional Forum, ADMM-Plus, maritime cooperation.
- Analyze economic role: AFTA, RCEP, supply-chain integration.
## 7. Data requirements

- ASEAN official documents and declarations
- ASEAN secretariat reports
- ASEAN-related think-tank studies (ISEAS, CSIS)
- Official MFA statements of member states
- News reporting on ASEAN summits
- Academic ASEAN studies
## 8. Approved public sources

- ASEAN
- ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute
- CSIS
- National MFAs
- Reuters/AP
- Academic literature
## 9. Source verification standards

- ASEAN statements reflect consensus, not unanimity of opinion; note this distinction. Avoid treating ASEAN as a
unitary actor — member states have divergent interests. Note that 'ASEAN centrality' is contested.
## 10. Analytical framework

- ASEAN-framework: analyze institutional design, assess internal cohesion, evaluate external partnerships, and assess effectiveness in security and economic governance.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating ASEAN consensus as genuine agreement.
- Ignoring member-state divergences.
- Overstating ASEAN's ability to act on security issues.
- Assuming external partnerships are cost-free.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in ASEAN internal manipulation or member-state targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate corruption, vote-buying, or covert influence over ASEAN processes.
## 16. Expected output

- An ASEAN brief: institutional analysis, cohesion assessment, partnership evaluation, effectiveness review,
confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Aspect defined
- Design analyzed
- Cohesion assessed
- Partnerships evaluated
- Effectiveness reviewed
- Confidence graded
