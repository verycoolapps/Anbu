# Strategic Forecasting
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Apply structured analytical techniques to forecast geopolitical developments — using indicators, models, and structured judgment to project future trajectories.
## 2. Scope

- Strategic forecasting: projecting geopolitical developments using structured techniques — indicators, models, expert judgment, and scenario analysis. Scenario planning is covered separately.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Select appropriate forecasting techniques for the question.
- Identify and track leading indicators.
- Apply structured judgment methods (Delphi, prediction markets, expert surveys).
- Generate probabilistic forecasts with confidence intervals.
## 4. Core concepts

- Leading indicators
- Structured judgment
- Probabilistic forecasting
- Forecast calibration
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Forecasting-framework: select techniques, identify indicators, apply structured judgment, generate probabilistic forecasts, evaluate accuracy, and update assessments.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the forecast question and timeframe.
- Select forecasting techniques: indicators, models, expert judgment, scenarios.
- Identify leading indicators: what predicts the outcome?
- Collect indicator data and assess current trends.
- Apply structured judgment: Delphi, expert surveys, prediction markets.
- Generate probabilistic forecasts with confidence intervals.

## 9. Source verification standards

- Forecasts are probabilistic, not deterministic; express uncertainty explicitly. Avoid false precision; use
ranges and confidence intervals. Evaluate forecasts against outcomes; note that forecasting is hard and often
wrong.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Forecasting-framework: select techniques, identify indicators, apply structured judgment, generate probabilistic forecasts, evaluate accuracy, and update assessments.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating forecasts as predictions rather than probabilities.
- Ignoring base rates and historical frequencies.
- Overfitting to recent trends.
- Failing to update forecasts with new information.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in forecast weaponization or manipulation.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate manipulation of prediction markets or expert surveys.
## 16. Expected output

- A forecasting report: technique selection, indicator analysis, probabilistic forecasts, confidence intervals,
accuracy evaluation.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Question defined
- Techniques selected
- Indicators identified
- Judgment applied
- Forecasts generated
- Accuracy evaluated
