# African Geopolitics Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze African geopolitics: great-power competition, regional organizations, resource politics, conflict patterns, and development-security nexus.
## 2. Scope

- African geopolitical dynamics: great-power engagement, regional organizations (AU, ECOWAS, etc.), resource politics, conflict patterns, and the development-security nexus.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map African geopolitical dynamics and key actors.
- Analyze great-power competition in Africa.
- Assess regional organizations and their effectiveness.
- Evaluate conflict patterns and resource politics.
## 4. Core concepts

- Great-power competition in Africa
- Regional organizations
- Resource politics
- Conflict patterns
- Development-security nexus
## 5. Analytical methodology

- African-geopolitics-framework: map actors and dynamics, analyze great-power competition, assess regional organizations, evaluate conflict patterns and resource politics.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the African scope (continental, regional, or country).
- Map key actors: AU, regional organizations, major powers, regional powers.
- Analyze great-power competition: US, China, Russia, EU engagement.
- Assess regional organizations: AU, ECOWAS, SADC effectiveness.
- Review conflict patterns: active conflicts, coups, terrorism.
- Analyze resource politics: minerals, oil, land, water.
## 7. Data requirements

- African Union and regional organization documents
- SIPRI and IISS assessments
- CSIS and RAND African studies
- World Bank and IMF Africa data
- Think-tank African analyses (ISS, Chatham House)
- News reporting on African conflicts

## 9. Source verification standards

- Africa is not a monolith; avoid treating it as a single geopolitical unit. Note that external engagement is
often framed as 'scramble' but is more complex. Distinguish state interests from non-state actors and local
dynamics.
## 10. Analytical framework

- African-geopolitics-framework: map actors and dynamics, analyze great-power competition, assess regional organizations, evaluate conflict patterns and resource politics.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating Africa as a single geopolitical unit.
- Overstating external power control vs local agency.
- Ignoring intra-state and sub-state dynamics.
- Assuming resource wealth determines politics.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in African conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on African actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- An African-geopolitics brief: actor map, competition analysis, organization assessment, conflict review,
resource analysis, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Actors mapped
- Competition analyzed
- Organizations assessed
- Conflicts reviewed
- Confidence graded
