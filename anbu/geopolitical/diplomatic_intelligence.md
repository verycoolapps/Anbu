# Diplomatic Intelligence Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Interpret the public diplomatic record — statements, protocols, appointments, summitry — to infer intentions, negotiating positions, and relationship temperature between states.
## 2. Scope

- Public diplomatic signals only: official statements, readouts, protocol choices, personnel moves, and summit choreography. It does not cover covert diplomacy or intelligence-service reporting.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Decode formal diplomatic signals: language choices, protocol breaks, appointment patterns.
- Infer negotiating positions and bottom lines from public statements and concessions.
- Track relationship temperature through diplomatic activity patterns.
- Identify back-channel indicators and signaling through intermediaries.
## 4. Core concepts

- Diplomatic signaling and protocol analysis
- Summit choreography
- Personnel as policy signal
- Track-one vs track-two channels
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Signal-intelligence framework: collect the diplomatic record, normalize it (what is said, how, to whom, when), compare against baseline behavior, and infer intent — always labeling inference distinctly from fact.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Build the diplomatic record for the dyad or issue over a defined window.
- Catalog statements: wording, venue, audience, timing.
- Note protocol signals: who meets whom, at what level, in what order.
- Track personnel changes in foreign ministries and missions.
- Identify intermediary and back-channel activity from public reporting.
- Synthesize intent assessments with explicit confidence grades.
## 7. Data requirements

- Official readouts and joint statements
- Foreign ministry websites and press conferences
- Summit communiqués
- Diplomatic appointment announcements
- Credible journalism on diplomacy
- Academic diplomatic-history scholarship
## 8. Approved public sources

- National MFAs
- UN
- Treaty secretariats
- Reuters/AP
- Think tanks (CSIS, Chatham House)
- Diplomatic-history scholarship
## 9. Source verification standards

- Protocol deviations are weak signals — verify against scheduling logistics before inferring intent. Quote
statements exactly; paraphrase only when labeled as such.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Signal-intelligence framework: collect the diplomatic record, normalize it (what is said, how, to whom, when), compare against baseline behavior, and infer intent — always labeling inference distinctly from fact.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Over-reading ritual language as substantive concession.
- Assuming silence means agreement rather than pending decision.
- Ignoring domestic audiences that statements are aimed at.
- Treating leaked 'diplomatic cables' of unverified provenance as fact.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no impersonation, fabrication of diplomatic communications, or covert channel operations.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not forge, alter, or fabricate diplomatic documents; do not intercept private
communications.
## 16. Expected output

- A diplomatic-signals brief: signal catalog, inferred positions, relationship temperature, intent assessments
with confidence.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Record window defined
- Statements quoted or precisely paraphrased
- Protocol signals noted
- Inferences labeled separately from facts
- Confidence graded
- Neutral tone maintained
