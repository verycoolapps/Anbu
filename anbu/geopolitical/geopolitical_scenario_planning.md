# Geopolitical Scenario Planning
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Develop and analyze geopolitical scenarios — structured narratives of alternative futures — to explore strategic uncertainties and test policy options.
## 2. Scope

- Scenario planning: developing alternative futures for geopolitical questions — covering drivers, uncertainties, scenario construction, and implications analysis. Forecasting is covered under strategic_forecasting.md.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Identify key drivers and uncertainties for a geopolitical question.
- Develop scenario narratives: baseline, optimistic, pessimistic, and wildcard.
- Analyze implications of each scenario for stakeholders.
- Test policy options against scenario outcomes.
## 4. Core concepts

- Scenario construction
- Driver analysis
- Uncertainty mapping
- Implication analysis
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Scenario-planning-framework: identify drivers and uncertainties, construct scenario narratives, analyze implications, test policy options, and communicate with explicit probabilities.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the geopolitical question and timeframe.
- Identify key drivers: political, economic, technological, social.
- Map uncertainties: what is unknown or contested?
- Construct scenarios: baseline, optimistic, pessimistic, wildcard.
- Analyze implications: who wins, who loses, what changes?
- Test policy options: which options work across scenarios?
## 7. Data requirements

- Think-tank scenario analyses (CSIS, RAND)
- Academic scenario-planning literature
- Official foresight and strategy documents
- News reporting on trend drivers
- Expert elicitation and Delphi studies
- Historical analogies and precedents
## 8. Approved public sources

- CSIS
- RAND
- Chatham House
- IISS
- Academic literature
- Expert elicitation
## 9. Source verification standards

- Scenarios are not predictions; they are tools for exploring uncertainty. Avoid false precision in
probabilities. Ensure scenarios are internally consistent and plausible. Note that scenario planning is only
as good as its drivers and uncertainties.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Scenario-planning-framework: identify drivers and uncertainties, construct scenario narratives, analyze implications, test policy options, and communicate with explicit probabilities.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating scenarios as predictions rather than explorations.
- Ignoring tail risks and wildcards.
- Assuming linear change rather than discontinuities.
- Overlooking second-order effects.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in scenario weaponization or disinformation.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate disinformation campaigns or manipulation of scenario narratives.
## 16. Expected output

- A scenario-planning report: driver analysis, scenario narratives, implication analysis, policy testing,
confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Question defined
- Drivers identified
- Uncertainties mapped
- Scenarios constructed
- Implications analyzed
- Confidence labeled
