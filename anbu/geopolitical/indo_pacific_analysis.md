# Indo-Pacific Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze the Indo-Pacific strategic environment: great-power competition, alliance networks, maritime disputes, economic interdependence, and regional institutions.
## 2. Scope

- The Indo-Pacific region: from South Asia through Southeast Asia to Northeast Asia and the Pacific — covering US–China competition, ASEAN centrality, maritime disputes, and economic integration.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map the Indo-Pacific strategic environment and its key actors.
- Analyze US–China competition and its regional effects.
- Assess alliance networks: US alliances, AUKUS, Quad, and regional partnerships.
- Evaluate maritime disputes and economic interdependence.
## 4. Core concepts

- Indo-Pacific strategy frameworks
- US–China strategic competition
- Alliance network mapping
- Maritime disputes and SLOCs
- Economic interdependence
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Indo-Pacific-framework: map actors and interests, analyze US–China competition, assess alliance and partnership networks, evaluate maritime and economic dynamics, and forecast regional trajectories.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the Indo-Pacific scope and timeframe.
- Map key actors: US, China, Japan, India, ASEAN, Australia, and others.
- Analyze US–China competition: military, economic, technological, institutional.
- Assess alliance networks: US alliances, Quad, AUKUS, regional partnerships.
- Evaluate maritime disputes: South China Sea, East China Sea, Taiwan Strait.
- Analyze economic interdependence: trade, supply chains, investment.
## 7. Data requirements

- SIPRI and IISS military assessments
- CSIS and RAND Indo-Pacific studies
- ASEAN and official MFA publications
- UN Comtrade and trade data
- Think-tank Indo-Pacific analyses
- News reporting on regional developments
## 8. Approved public sources

- SIPRI

## 9. Source verification standards

- Indo-Pacific is a strategic construct, not a geographic given — note different definitions. Avoid framing
analysis as zero-sum; many states hedge. Distinguish rhetoric from capability.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Indo-Pacific-framework: map actors and interests, analyze US–China competition, assess alliance and partnership networks, evaluate maritime and economic dynamics, and forecast regional trajectories.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming Indo-Pacific is a unified bloc.
- Treating US–China competition as the only dynamic.
- Ignoring ASEAN's agency and centrality debates.
- Overstating alliance cohesion or rivalry inevitability.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in Indo-Pacific conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on regional actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- An Indo-Pacific brief: actor map, competition analysis, alliance assessment, dispute register, economic
interdependence, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Actors mapped
- Competition analyzed
- Alliances assessed
- Disputes reviewed
- Confidence graded
