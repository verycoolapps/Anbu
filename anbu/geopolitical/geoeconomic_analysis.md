# Geoeconomic Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze the intersection of economics and geopolitics: economic tools of statecraft, geoeconomic competition, and the strategic effects of economic interdependence.
## 2. Scope

- Geoeconomic statecraft: sanctions, trade weapons, investment screening, economic coercion, and the strategic use of economic interdependence. Pure economics is out of scope.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Identify geoeconomic tools and their strategic purposes.
- Analyze economic interdependence as both constraint and weapon.
- Assess geoeconomic competition between major powers.
- Evaluate the effectiveness and limits of geoeconomic statecraft.
## 4. Core concepts

- Geoeconomic statecraft
- Economic interdependence weaponization
- Strategic decoupling
- Economic coercion vs persuasion
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Geoeconomic-framework: catalog economic tools, assess interdependence asymmetries, analyze weaponization cases, and evaluate effectiveness and limits of geoeconomic strategies.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the geoeconomic question or state pair.
- Catalog economic tools: sanctions, tariffs, investment screening, aid, debt.
- Assess interdependence asymmetries: who needs whom more?
- Review weaponization cases: sanctions, trade wars, tech restrictions.
- Evaluate effectiveness: did coercion work? What were the costs?
- Analyze limits: adaptation, evasion, third-party effects.
## 7. Data requirements

- WTO and trade data
- Sanctions lists (OFAC, EU, UN)
- Investment screening regulations
- World Bank and IMF data
- Think-tank geoeconomic studies (CSIS, RAND)
- Academic geoeconomics literature
## 8. Approved public sources

- WTO
- UN
- World Bank
- IMF
- CSIS
- RAND
- OFAC/EU/UN sanctions bodies
## 9. Source verification standards

- Geoeconomic tools have second-order effects; analyze them. Distinguish intended effects from actual outcomes.
Note that interdependence can constrain as well as enable coercion.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Geoeconomic-framework: catalog economic tools, assess interdependence asymmetries, analyze weaponization cases, and evaluate effectiveness and limits of geoeconomic strategies.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating economic tools as cost-free.
- Ignoring adaptation and evasion responses.
- Assuming interdependence always constrains conflict.
- Missing third-party effects and coalition dynamics.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in sanctions evasion or economic warfare.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate sanctions evasion, trade fraud, or economic sabotage.
## 16. Expected output

- A geoeconomic brief: tool catalog, interdependence assessment, weaponization cases, effectiveness evaluation,
confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Question defined
- Tools cataloged
- Asymmetries assessed
- Cases reviewed
- Effectiveness evaluated
- Confidence graded
