# Maritime Geopolitics Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze maritime competition: naval postures, sea lanes, chokepoints, bases, and maritime disputes — the domain where great-power rivalry most often becomes physical.
## 2. Scope

- Naval affairs and maritime strategy: fleets, bases, sea lanes of communication (SLOCs), chokepoints, submarine warfare, and maritime domain awareness.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map naval forces, basing networks, and power-projection reach.
- Identify critical sea lanes and chokepoints and their vulnerability.
- Analyze maritime disputes: EEZ claims, island disputes, freedom of navigation.
- Assess the maritime balance and its trajectory.
## 4. Core concepts

- Sea lines of communication
- Chokepoint vulnerability
- Anti-access/area denial
- Maritime domain awareness
- Naval basing networks
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Maritime-balance framework: inventory fleets and bases, map SLOC dependence, assess chokepoint exposure, evaluate A2/AD capabilities, and grade the regional maritime balance.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the maritime theater and time horizon.
- Collect fleet inventories: combatants, submarines, auxiliaries, bases.
- Map SLOCs and chokepoints: trade dependence, alternative routes, vulnerability.
- Assess A2/AD systems: missiles, sensors, submarines, cyber.
- Evaluate maritime disputes and their escalation potential.
- Produce the maritime balance assessment with confidence.
## 7. Data requirements

- IISS Military Balance and naval assessments
- SIPRI arms transfers
- Official navy publications and doctrine
- AIS and maritime tracking data (public)
- Think-tank maritime studies (CSIS, RAND)
- UNCLOS and maritime treaty texts
## 8. Approved public sources

- IISS
- SIPRI
- UN (UNCLOS, ITLOS)
- National navies
- CSIS
- RAND
- Maritime-tracking data providers
## 9. Source verification standards

- Fleet numbers are public; operational readiness often is not — mark readiness claims as assessments.
Distinguish commissioned capability from latent capacity.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Maritime-balance framework: inventory fleets and bases, map SLOC dependence, assess chokepoint exposure, evaluate A2/AD capabilities, and grade the regional maritime balance.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Counting hulls without assessing capability and readiness.
- Treating sea lanes as fixed rather than adaptable.
- Ignoring submarine (quiet) forces in balance assessments.
- Overlooking coast guards and maritime militia in gray-zone competition.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no targeting of vessels or assistance to maritime interdiction operations.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate piracy, sanctions evasion at sea, or targeting of shipping.
## 16. Expected output

- A maritime assessment: fleet and base map, SLOC/chokepoint analysis, A2/AD evaluation, dispute register,
balance grade.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Theater defined
- Fleets and bases inventoried
- SLOCs and chokepoints mapped
- Disputes assessed
- Balance graded
- Confidence stated
