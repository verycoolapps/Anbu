# Geopolitical Risk Matrix
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Construct and apply geopolitical risk matrices — systematic frameworks for scoring and ranking country, regional, and issue-level geopolitical risks.
## 2. Scope

- Risk assessment frameworks: country risk, regional risk, issue-specific risk — covering political, security, economic, and operational dimensions. Scenario planning is covered separately.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Define risk dimensions and scoring criteria.
- Collect indicators for each risk dimension.
- Score and rank risks using a consistent methodology.
- Apply the matrix to specific countries, regions, or issues.
## 4. Core concepts

- Risk dimensions
- Scoring methodology
- Indicator selection
- Confidence labeling
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Risk-matrix-framework: define dimensions, select indicators, establish scoring criteria, collect data, score risks, rank results, and communicate with explicit uncertainty.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the risk scope: country, region, or issue.
- Select risk dimensions: political, security, economic, operational, reputational.
- Choose indicators for each dimension with clear definitions.
- Establish scoring criteria: scales, weights, thresholds.
- Collect data for each indicator from reliable sources.
- Score and rank risks; apply confidence labels.
## 7. Data requirements

- World Bank and IMF risk indicators
- SIPRI and IISS security data
- UN and treaty data
- Think-tank risk assessments (EIU, Control Risks)
- News reporting on risk events
- Academic risk-assessment literature
## 8. Approved public sources

## 9. Source verification standards

- Risk matrices are tools, not predictions; note their limitations. Distinguish likelihood from impact. Avoid
false precision; use ranges rather than point estimates where uncertainty is high.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Risk-matrix-framework: define dimensions, select indicators, establish scoring criteria, collect data, score risks, rank results, and communicate with explicit uncertainty.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating risk scores as predictions rather than assessments.
- Ignoring correlation between risk dimensions.
- Assuming static risk rather than dynamic.
- Overlooking tail risks and black swans.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in risk exploitation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on high-risk actors or exploitation of vulnerabilities.
## 16. Expected output

- A risk-matrix report: dimension definitions, scoring methodology, results, confidence labels, caveats.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Dimensions selected
- Indicators chosen
- Scoring criteria established
- Data collected
- Results communicated with caveats
