# Geopolitical Rivalry Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze enduring rivalries between states or blocs — their causes, drivers, flashpoints, and stability — to forecast escalation paths and identify off-ramps.
## 2. Scope

- Inter-state rivalries at systemic and regional scale: US–China, India–Pakistan, Greece–Turkey-type dyads, and emergent blocs. Internal insurgencies are out of scope.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Define the rivalry dyad and its historical trajectory.
- Identify structural drivers (power transition, territorial, ideological, economic) vs contingent triggers.
- Map recurring flashpoints and escalation ladders.
- Assess stability: risks of war, managed competition, or durable accommodation.
## 4. Core concepts

- Structural vs contingent causes
- Escalation ladders
- Security dilemma
- Compellence and deterrence dynamics
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Rivalry-lifecycle framework: reconstruct the rivalry's origin and reinforcing mechanisms, identify flashpoint geography, model escalation paths, and evaluate stabilizers (deterrence, institutions, economic interdependence, communication channels).
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Reconstruct the rivalry's history from primary sources; note turning points.
- Classify causes as structural or contingent.
- Enumerate flashpoints and rank by salience and frequency.
- Build an escalation ladder: incidents, mobilization, alliance activation, conflict.
- Assess stabilizers and their credibility; assign a stability grade.
- Present competing hypotheses for near-term trajectory.
## 7. Data requirements

- SIPRI arms transfer and military expenditure data
- Official crisis communiqués and incident records
- Treaty and confidence-building agreement texts
- Think-tank rivalry studies (CSIS, RAND, IISS)
- Declassified diplomatic histories where public
- Academic conflict databases
## 8. Approved public sources

- UN
- SIPRI
- CSIS
- RAND
- IISS
- Chatham House
- Official MFA statements
- Government crisis records
## 9. Source verification standards

- Distinguish incident facts (verified by multiple parties or imagery) from each side's narrative. Weight
adversary statements for intent, own-side statements for policy.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Rivalry-lifecycle framework: reconstruct the rivalry's origin and reinforcing mechanisms, identify flashpoint geography, model escalation paths, and evaluate stabilizers (deterrence, institutions, economic interdependence, communication channels).
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming rivalry is permanent rather than cyclical.
- Treating propaganda claims as evidence of capability.
- Ignoring economic interdependence that constrains escalation.
- Focusing on leaders while ignoring bureaucratic and military incentives.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only. No targeting support, no escalation advice usable for operational planning.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not assist covert action, disinformation, or influence campaigns.
## 16. Expected output

- A rivalry dossier: drivers, flashpoints, escalation ladder, stabilizers, stability grade, and scenario
branches with confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Dyad and timeframe defined
- Structural vs contingent causes separated
- Escalation ladder explicit
- Stabilizers assessed
- Both sides' perspectives represented neutrally
- Confidence graded
