# Southeast Asia Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze Southeast Asia's strategic environment: ASEAN centrality, great-power competition, maritime disputes, economic integration, and internal political dynamics.
## 2. Scope

- The Southeast Asian region: ASEAN member states and their relations with major powers — covering maritime disputes, economic integration, and regional security.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map Southeast Asia's strategic position and key actors.
- Analyze ASEAN's role and internal cohesion.
- Assess great-power competition effects on the region.
- Evaluate maritime disputes and economic integration.
## 4. Core concepts

- ASEAN centrality
- Great-power competition effects
- Maritime disputes
- Economic integration
- Internal political dynamics
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Southeast-Asia-framework: map actors and interests, analyze ASEAN's role and cohesion, assess great-power competition effects, evaluate maritime disputes and economic integration.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the Southeast Asian scope.
- Map key actors: ASEAN members, major powers, external partners.
- Analyze ASEAN: membership, decision-making, centrality claims, internal divisions.
- Assess great-power competition: US–China rivalry effects on the region.
- Evaluate maritime disputes: South China Sea claimants and dynamics.
- Analyze economic integration: RCEP, CPTPP, supply chains.

## 8. Approved public sources

- ASEAN

## 9. Source verification standards

- ASEAN is consensus-based and non-interventionist; note that this shapes its strategic role. Avoid treating
Southeast Asia as a monolith — states have diverse interests. Distinguish claims from capabilities in maritime
disputes.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Southeast-Asia-framework: map actors and interests, analyze ASEAN's role and cohesion, assess great-power competition effects, evaluate maritime disputes and economic integration.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating ASEAN as a unified actor.
- Assuming great-power competition determines all regional dynamics.
- Ignoring internal political changes in member states.
- Overstating maritime dispute escalation risk.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in regional conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on regional actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- A Southeast Asia brief: actor map, ASEAN analysis, competition effects, dispute assessment, economic
integration, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Actors mapped
- ASEAN analyzed
- Competition effects assessed
- Disputes reviewed
- Confidence graded
