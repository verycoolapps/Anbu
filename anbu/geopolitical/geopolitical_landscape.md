# Geopolitical Landscape Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Provide a structured, evidence-graded assessment of the global distribution of power, alignment, and strategic tension that frames every other ANBU division.
## 2. Scope

- Systemic international conditions: power distribution, alliance systems, conflict zones, and the strategic environment in which states act. Excludes country-internal politics (see political/) and detailed force posture (see military/).
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map the current distribution of material and institutional power across major states and blocs.
- Identify the principal alignment structures and the forces pulling them together or apart.
- Locate active and latent conflict zones and rank them by escalation risk.
- Establish the baseline strategic environment used by downstream division files.
## 4. Core concepts

- Power distribution
- Alliance systems
- Strategic enablers and constraints
- Systemic vs unit-level analysis
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Power-transition and systemic-stability frameworks: assess unipolar remnants, bipolar competition, and multipolar fragmentation; locate each state on a power spectrum (hegemon, great power, middle power, small state).
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the analytical timeframe and geographic boundary.
- Collect power indicators: SIPRI military expenditure, defense budgets, alliance membership, institutional leadership roles.
- Map active conflicts, frozen disputes, and emerging flashpoints; note escalation triggers.
- Assess alignment structures: formal alliances, ad-hoc coalitions, rival blocs, and non-aligned groupings.
- Synthesize the landscape narrative; grade every major claim.
- Issue the report with a baseline assessment valid for a stated period.
## 7. Data requirements

- SIPRI military expenditure database
- UN membership and treaty records
- Alliance communiqués and treaty texts
- World Bank and IMF structural data
- CSIS, IISS, RAND, Chatham House assessments
- Official defense ministry and MFA publications
## 8. Approved public sources

- World Bank
- IMF
- SIPRI
- UN
- NATO and alliance secretariats
- CSIS
- RAND
- IISS
## 9. Source verification standards

- Cross-check every load-bearing claim against at least two independent source classes (primary document plus
secondary analysis). Date-stamp all data; flag any indicator older than 24 months.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Power-transition and systemic-stability frameworks: assess unipolar remnants, bipolar competition, and multipolar fragmentation; locate each state on a power spectrum (hegemon, great power, middle power, small state).
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Treating military spending as synonymous with national power without accounting for economic base and geography.
- Assuming alliance labels imply unified behavior during crises.
- Ignoring non-state actors and sub-state dynamics that reshape the landscape.
- Presenting a snapshot as a trend without temporal data.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no recommendations that could enable offensive operations or target selection. Non-partisan
framing of all state actors.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not compile targeting packages, do not name individuals for action, do not assist
weapons programs or sanctions evasion.
## 16. Expected output

- A graded landscape brief containing: power distribution summary, alignment map, conflict-zone register,
escalation-risk ranking, and an explicit confidence statement.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Timeframe and boundary stated
- Every claim carries an evidence label
- Power indicators dated and sourced
- Conflicting assessments presented where they exist
- Confidence graded HIGH/MODERATE/LOW
- No advocacy or operational recommendations
- Cross-references to core and other geopolitical files included
