# Bilateral Relations Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Assess the state of relations between two states across political, economic, security, and people-to-people dimensions, identifying friction points and cooperation channels.
## 2. Scope

- The bilateral relationship between two specific states. Multilateral frameworks and alliance politics are covered in related files.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Grade the overall temperature and trajectory of a bilateral relationship.
- Decompose relations into functional domains (political, trade, security, consular, cultural).
- Identify friction points, negotiation channels, and domestic constraints on each side.
- Forecast near-term trajectory under stated assumptions.
## 4. Core concepts

- Relationship temperature and trajectory
- Functional decomposition
- Friction vs cooperation inventory
- Domestic constraint mapping
## 5. Analytical methodology

- Relationship-assessment framework: score each functional domain, weight by strategic salience, map friction points and dialogue mechanisms, account for domestic politics on both sides, and extrapolate trajectory.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the dyad and timeframe; gather relationship baseline.
- Collect diplomatic history: summits, joint statements, recalled ambassadors, sanctions.
- Score functional domains using public indicators (trade volumes, treaty status, military incidents).
- Map friction points to specific issues and domestic constituencies.
- Assess dialogue channels and their current health.
- Produce a graded bilateral assessment with trajectory forecast.
## 7. Data requirements

- Official joint statements and communiqués
- Trade and investment statistics (UN Comtrade, national statistics)
- Sanctions lists (OFAC, EU, UN)
- Consular and visa policy changes
- Think-tank bilateral studies
- News reporting from credible outlets
## 8. Approved public sources

- UN Comtrade
- World Bank
- IMF
- UN sanctions committees
- National MFAs
- CSIS
- Chatham House
- Reuters/AP reporting
## 9. Source verification standards

- Joint statements are evidence of what was said, not of agreement reached — verify implementation through
subsequent actions. Treat social-media diplomacy as rhetoric, not policy.
## 10. Analytical framework

- Relationship-assessment framework: score each functional domain, weight by strategic salience, map friction points and dialogue mechanisms, account for domestic politics on both sides, and extrapolate trajectory.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Inferring relationship warmth from rhetoric volume.
- Ignoring asymmetry: one side's concession may be routine for the other.
- Missing quiet back-channels documented in memoirs or leaks.
- Assuming continuity when leadership or coalition changes alter incentives.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only. Do not provide guidance on influencing bilateral outcomes covertly.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. No collection on private citizens; no facilitation of espionage or covert diplomacy.
## 16. Expected output

- A bilateral relations brief: domain scores, friction-point register, dialogue-channel map, trajectory
forecast, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Dyad defined
- Domains scored with evidence
- Friction points tied to specific issues
- Trajectory forecast with assumptions stated
- Neutral framing of both sides
- Confidence graded
