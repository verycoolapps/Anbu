# European Security Analysis
**Division:** Geopolitical | **Type:** Analytical Reference | **Version:** 1.0
## 1. Purpose

- Analyze European security: NATO's role, EU strategic autonomy, Russian threat perception, defense spending, and the evolving European security architecture.
## 2. Scope

- European security dynamics: NATO, EU defense initiatives, Russian–Western tensions, and the post-2022 security order. Transatlantic relations broadly are covered under alliance files.
## 3. Intelligence objectives

- Map the European security architecture and its key institutions.
- Analyze NATO's role, cohesion, and burden-sharing.
- Assess EU strategic autonomy initiatives and their limits.
- Evaluate Russian threat perception and European responses.
## 4. Core concepts

- NATO cohesion and burden-sharing
- EU strategic autonomy
- Russian threat perception
- European defense industrial base
- Transatlantic relations
## 5. Analytical methodology

- European-security-framework: map institutions, analyze NATO cohesion, assess EU autonomy initiatives, evaluate Russian threat and responses, and forecast European security trajectories.
## 6. Step-by-step workflow

- Define the European security scope.
- Map key institutions: NATO, EU, OSCE, national defense ministries.
- Analyze NATO: membership, Article 5, defense spending, cohesion.
- Assess EU strategic autonomy: defense initiatives, industrial policy, capability gaps.
- Evaluate Russian threat: military posture, hybrid warfare, nuclear signaling.
- Analyze European responses: defense spending, energy diversification, institutional reform.
## 7. Data requirements

- NATO official documents and communiqués
- EU defense and security documents
- SIPRI and IISS assessments
- CSIS and RAND European studies
- Official defense ministry publications
- News reporting on European security
## 8. Approved public sources

- NATO

- National defense ministries
## 9. Source verification standards

- European security is not monolithic; note East-West and North-South divides. Avoid treating Russia as the only
threat; note terrorism, migration, and hybrid challenges. Distinguish capability from aspiration in EU
defense.
## 10. Analytical framework

- European-security-framework: map institutions, analyze NATO cohesion, assess EU autonomy initiatives, evaluate Russian threat and responses, and forecast European security trajectories.
## 11. Evidence requirements

- Use the ANBU seven-label evidence vocabulary (FACT, VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, ASSESSMENT, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS,
SPECULATION). Every claim must carry an evidence label. Load-bearing claims require corroboration. Inferences
must be logically derivable from cited evidence. Speculation must be explicitly labeled and confined to
clearly marked sections.
## 12. Confidence assessment

- Assess confidence on three dimensions: source confidence (trustworthiness of origin), information confidence
(reliability of the specific item), and analytical confidence (soundness of the inference). Report all three.
Use HIGH (multiple corroborated sources, strong analytical basis), MODERATE (single reliable source or limited
corroboration), LOW (weak sourcing or significant analytical uncertainty), INSUFFICIENT (inadequate evidence
to support assessment).
## 13. Common analytical mistakes

- Assuming European unity on security.
- Treating EU strategic autonomy as imminent rather than aspirational.
- Ignoring NATO's enduring role.
- Overstating or understating Russian capabilities.
## 14. Ethical considerations

- Analysis only; no assistance in European conflict escalation or targeting.
## 15. Security boundaries

- Public sources only. Do not facilitate attacks on European actors or assistance to armed groups.
## 16. Expected output

- A European-security brief: institutional map, NATO analysis, EU autonomy assessment, Russian threat
evaluation, confidence labels.
## 17. Quality checklist

- Scope defined
- Institutions mapped
- NATO analyzed
- EU autonomy assessed
- Russian threat evaluated
- Confidence graded
